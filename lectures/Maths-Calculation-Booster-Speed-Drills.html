∛ Cube Root of Perfect Cubes (Page 8)

Unique Unit Digit Rule for Cubes:

Every digit from 0 to 9 has a unique unit digit in its cube:

  • 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 9 → Same unit digit (0→0, 1→1, 4→4, 5→5, 6→6, 9→9)
  • 2 ↔ 8 (2³ = 8, 8³ = 512)  and  3 ↔ 7 (3³ = 27, 7³ = 343)
  • Method: Strike out the last 3 digits after matching the unit digit, then find the largest cube ≤ the remaining prefix!

8. Properties of Perfect Squares & Exam Questions (Q.1–Q.9)

Pages 10–12 • 5 Elimination Filters for Perfect Squares + 10 Exam Questions

🏆 5 Golden Properties of a Perfect Square Number (Page 10)

  1. Unit Digit Filter: The unit digit of a perfect square can NEVER be 2, 3, 7, or 8 (it must be 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, or 9).
  2. Last 2 Digits Filter: The last two digits of ANY perfect square must match the last two digits of one of the squares from 1² to 25² (e.g., 00, 01, 04, 09, 16, 21, 24, 25, 29, 36, 41, 44, 49, 56, 61, 64, 69, 76, 81, 84, 89, 96).
    • Special Corollary: If a perfect square ends in 5, its tens digit is always 2 (ends in 25). If it ends in 6, its tens digit must be odd; otherwise, its tens digit is always even!
  3. Digital Sum (Root Sum) Filter: The digital sum of a perfect square is always 1, 4, 7, or 9.
  4. Remainder on Dividing by 8: When divided by 8, a perfect square always leaves a remainder of 0, 1, or 4.
  5. Even Number of Zeroes / Decimal Places: A rational perfect square must have an even number of trailing zeroes after the last non-zero digit, and an even number of digits after the decimal point.
