⚙️ Mechanics & Dynamics

Newton's Laws of Motion & Gravitation

  • First Law (Law of Inertia): A body remains at rest or in uniform motion along a straight line unless acted upon by an external resultant force.
  • Second Law: The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the force (F = ma).
  • Third Law: To every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
  • Newton's Law of Gravitation: The gravitational force between two point masses is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance separating them:
    F = G m1 m2r2

Free-Fall Kinematics & Tower Height PYQ Solution

Kinematic Equations for Free-Fall (u = 0 m/s):

v = gt • h = 12gt2 • v2 = 2gh
📌 PYQ Mathematical Solution — Tower Height

Problem: Calculate the height of a tower if a dropped ball touches the ground in t = 10 seconds.

• Candidate Verbal Etiquette: Before stating your final calculated height to the panel, explicitly state your assumed value for g.

• Calculation (assuming g = 9.8 m/s2): h = 12 × 9.8 × (10)2 = 0.5 × 9.8 × 100 = 490 meters

• Calculation (assuming g = 10 m/s2): h = 12 × 10 × 100 = 500 meters

🎖️ Captain's Evaluation Note

In oral examinations, candidates often lose points not because their arithmetic is wrong, but because they blurt out "500 meters" without stating that they assumed g = 10 m/s2. Always declare your constants first—it demonstrates disciplined engineering protocol.

Rotational Dynamics, Escape Velocity & Curved Motion

  • Escape Velocity: Minimum vertical velocity required for a body to overcome a celestial body's gravitational field completely: ve = √2gR ≈ 11.2 km/s (for Earth).
  • Centripetal Force: Inward force required to constrain a mass to a curved path (Fc = mv2r).
  • Centrifugal Force: Apparent, inertia-driven outward force experienced within a rotating frame of reference.
  • Physics of a Cyclist Bending on Turns: A cyclist leaning inward at an angle θ from the vertical creates a horizontal component of the normal contact force (N sin θ) that provides the exact centripetal force (mv2/r) required to execute the turn without skidding laterally.

Elasticity Mechanics (Steel vs. Rubber Ball PYQ)

Y = StressStrain = F / AΔL / L0
📌 PYQ Analysis: Steel Ball vs. Rubber Ball

A steel ball is more elastic than a rubber ball. Steel possesses a significantly higher Young's Modulus (Y) than rubber, meaning steel requires a far greater deforming stress to produce the same strain, and generates a much stronger internal restoring force to regain its original geometry.

🌊 Fluid Mechanics & Hydrostatics

Hydrostatic Pressure & Fluid Density

  • Fluid Density: ρ = mV (Unit: kg/m3).
  • Hydrostatic Pressure Equation: P = ρgh.
  • Absolute Pressure at Dam Wall Base:
    Pbottom = Patm + ρgh
    Where ρ represents stored water density (≈ 1000 kg/m3) and h is vertical water depth.

Bernoulli's Principle & Aerodynamic Lift

Bernoulli's Theorem: For laminar, incompressible, non-viscous fluid flow, total mechanical energy remains constant along a streamline:

P + 12ρv2 + ρgz = Constant

Aerodynamic Lift: Air travels faster over the curved upper surface of an airfoil than beneath the flat lower surface. Elevated fluid velocity over the top reduces static pressure, producing a net upward aerodynamic lift force.

Archimedes' Principle, Buoyancy & Vessel Flotation

  • Archimedes' Principle: A body immersed wholly or partially in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body.
  • Iron Needle vs. Steel Ship: A solid iron needle sinks because its structural density (≈ 7850 kg/m3) exceeds water density (≈ 1000 kg/m3). A steel ship floats because its hollowed hull encloses vast air space; its overall mean displacement density (Total Mass / Total Enclosed Volume) is less than water, displacing a weight of water equal to its total displacement mass.
  • Partially Submerged Steel Bucket: The weight of the submerged portion of the bucket plus its contents is exactly equal to the weight of the liquid displaced by that submerged volume.

Capillarity & Surface Tension Mechanics

  • Surface Tension: Cohesive intermolecular forces pulling liquid molecules inward at an interface, causing the surface layer to behave like a stretched elastic membrane (Unit: N/m).
  • Capillary Action: Spontaneous movement of a liquid through narrow channels driven by adhesive forces between liquid and solid surfaces exceeding internal cohesive forces.
  • Oil Lamp Mechanism: Fuel ascends a woven cotton wick via capillary action through microscopic interstitial spaces between fabric strands.
  • Paper on Wet Table: Laying dry paper on a wet table engages strong adhesive molecular forces between liquid and paper cellulose fibers, while liquid surface tension pulls the paper flat onto the table.

Quantum Mechanics & Wave Optics (Interference vs. Diffraction)

  • Planck's Constant (h): h = 6.626 × 10−34 J·s (or kg·m2/s).
  • De Broglie Hypothesis: Matter exhibits dual wave-particle properties: λ = hp = hmv
  • Interference: Superposition of light waves from two coherent, distinct sources, producing uniform fringe widths and equal intensity maxima.
  • Diffraction: Bending of light waves around sharp obstacle edges or narrow slits, producing non-uniform fringe widths and rapidly decaying intensity maxima.

Gas Laws & Thermal Physics

  • Ideal Gas Law: PV = nRT (R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)).
  • Boyle's Law (Const T): P1V1 = P2V2
  • Charles' Law (Const P): V1T1 = V2T2
  • Gay-Lussac's (Const V): P1T1 = P2T2
  • Latent Heat: Q = mL (phase change at constant T).
  • Linear Expansion: α = ΔLL0 ΔT (K−1).

Boiling, Anomalous Water & Condensation

  • Pressure Cooker: Elevated internal pressure raises water's boiling point above 100°C (≈ 120°C at 2 atm).
  • Boiling Water at 5°C: Using a vacuum pump to drop ambient vessel pressure to 0.87 kPa (0.0087 bar) makes water boil at 5°C.
  • Anomalous Expansion: Water reaches maximum density (1000 kg/m3) at 4°C; ice at 0°C is less dense (≈ 917 kg/m3).
  • Cold Glass Condensation: Ice cools glass below ambient air's dew point, condensing water vapor into droplets.

Geophysical & Astronomical PYQs

  • High-Altitude Drop: Lower partial O2 pressure causes hypoxia; lower ambient pressure lowers boiling points and alters pipe head pressure (causing air-locks).

Inaccessible Mountain Peak Height Calculation

Problem: Calculate the vertical height h of an inaccessible mountain peak without knowing the distance to its base.

  • Method: Measure initial angle of elevation α at Position A. Walk a measured horizontal distance d toward the peak to Position B and measure new elevation angle β (β > α).
  • Derivation: cot α = Xh and cot β = X − dh.
  • Subtracting Equations Yields:
cot α − cot β = dh  ⇒  h = dcot α − cot β

Time, Speed & Distance Overtake PYQ

Problem: A leaves at 1000 hrs at 3 km/h. B leaves same origin at 1200 hrs at 9 km/h. When will B overtake A?

1. Head Start (2 hrs): 3 km/h × 2 h = 6 km
2. Relative Speed: 9 − 3 = 6 km/h
3. Catch-up Time: 6 km / 6 km/h = 1 hr
4. Meeting Time: 1200 + 1 hr = 1300 hrs (1:00 PM)

Furthermore, advanced hull coatings and energy-saving propulsion devices reduce vessel fuel consumption, directly lowering greenhouse gas output per ton-mile of cargo transported.

In conclusion, combating global warming and ocean acidification requires continuous global environmental action and sustainable industrial practices. Enforcing strict vessel emission controls and transitioning to clean alternative fuels ensures that international shipping supports global trade while safeguarding marine ecosystems for future generations. 🔬 4.2. High-Frequency Technical & Applied Physics Interview PYQs
Tech Q1 "Explain how a massive steel ship floats without using Archimedes' Principle or the word 'Buoyancy'."
"Sir, flotation can be explained by analyzing total enclosed geometry and mean mass density. A ship's hull is built as a large, hollow steel structure enclosing a massive volume of empty air space. The overall mean density of the vessel is calculated as ρmean = Total Structural Mass / Total Enclosed Hull Volume. Because air density is roughly 1.2 kg/m3, the ship's overall mean density is significantly lower than surrounding sea water (≈ 1025 kg/m3). Downward gravity acting on the ship's total mass is balanced by the upward static hydrostatic fluid pressure exerted by the ocean against the submerged hull plates, holding the vessel in stable equilibrium."
Tech Q2 "How is oxygen generated inside a submerged submarine during extended underwater missions?"
"Primary oxygen is generated onboard a submerged submarine through continuous water electrolysis. Electrical power supplied by the submarine's generator passes through an automated oxygen generator unit, splitting purified fresh water into hydrogen and oxygen gas (2H2O → 2H2↑ + O2↑). The oxygen is piped into the ventilation system, while hydrogen is safely discharged overboard. For emergency backup, submarines carry solid chlorate oxygen candles containing sodium chlorate (NaClO3) and iron powder, which release oxygen through a self-sustaining chemical reaction when thermally ignited."
Tech Q3 "Why are air conditioners not used in high-altitude mountain regions?"
"Sir, high-altitude mountain regions already feature low ambient temperatures, making space cooling unnecessary. Furthermore, atmospheric air density decreases with altitude. Standard air conditioner condenser units rely on dense ambient air to absorb rejected heat; lower air density reduces condenser heat-exchange efficiency."
Tech Q4 "Why do evaporative water coolers work in dry summer but fail during the monsoon?"
"Water coolers operate on evaporative cooling, where water absorbs latent heat of vaporization from surrounding air. Summer air has low relative humidity, allowing rapid evaporation. Monsoon air is saturated with water vapor (high relative humidity), preventing evaporation."
Tech Q5 "A man wears a green shirt into a dark room illuminated purely by blue light. What color will the shirt appear?"
"The green shirt will appear black, Sir. Green pigment reflects green wavelengths while absorbing all others. Under pure blue light, the green pigment absorbs the incident blue wavelength, leaving no reflected light to reach the observer's eye."
Tech Q6 "Which gives better thermal insulation: one thick wool blanket or two thin blankets layered together?"
"Two thin blankets layered together provide superior insulation, Captain. Layering traps a pocket of still air between them. Since still air is a poor thermal conductor, it drastically reduces conductive and convective heat loss."
Tech Q7 "Why does the cylinder of a manual bicycle pump heat up rapidly while inflating a tire?"
"Pushing the piston performs rapid mechanical work compressing the air inside. Under the First Law of Thermodynamics, rapid adiabatic compression increases the gas's internal thermal kinetic energy before heat can dissipate."
Tech Q8 "Why are condensation droplets seen on the outer surface of a glass containing ice cubes?"
"Ice cubes cool the outer glass surface below the dew point of surrounding air. Atmospheric water vapor colliding with the cold glass loses thermal kinetic energy and condenses into liquid droplets."

4 Risk Assessment Levels & Management of Change (MOC)

  • Level 1 (Generic Company RA): Shore-based SMS baseline policies.
  • Level 2 (Task-Based RA - TBRA): Shipboard officer/crew assessment prior to high-risk tasks (hot work, enclosed space, work aloft).
