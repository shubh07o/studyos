Exhaustive Notes
⚖️ Adjective vs Determiner
Foundation
Adjectives Determiners
1. Can be used as complement 1. Can't be used as complement
2. Gradable (wiser, wisest) 2. Non-gradable
3. Adverbs can qualify them 3. Adverbs cannot qualify them
🎭 Attributive vs Predicative
Usage Types

Attributive: Placed directly before Noun (I met a brave soldier.)

Predicative: Used after linking verb as complement without noun (The soldier is brave.)

Linking Verbs taking Adjectives: Feel, taste, smell, sound, look, seem, appear, become, be, turn, get, grow, keep, make, prove.
📊 The Three Degrees of Comparison
Core Structure
Positive Degree

Represents equality or basic quality:

• As + Positive + As (Affirmative & Negative)

• So + Positive + As (Negative only)

Ex: She is as intelligent as her classmates.

Comparative Degree

Comparison between two entities:

• er / more + than

• The + comparative + of the two (Selection)

Ex: Ramesh is taller than Suresh.

Superlative Degree

One compared against all:

• The + -est / most

• No 'other' or 'else' allowed!

Ex: Rahul is the best player of the team.

🔍 High-Yield Confusing Pairs Mastered
Exam Golden Rules
Elder / Eldest vs Older / Oldest

• Elder / Eldest: Strictly for blood relations (family members). Never takes 'than'. (My elder brother is a teacher.)

• Older / Oldest: For non-family persons or objects; takes 'than'. (Rohan is older than his friend.)

Further vs Farther

• Farther: Physical distance (Mumbai is farther from Patna than Ambala.)

• Further: Additional / subsequent (I need further support / further discussion.)

Later / Latter vs Latest / Last

• Later / Latest: Time oriented (He came later than you. What is the latest score?)

• Latter / Last: Order / position oriented (Latter: second of two; Last: end of series.)

• Former vs First: Former = first of two; First = first of 3+.

Latin Comparatives & Prefer

• Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, anterior, posterior:

Always take to (NEVER 'than'). Never precede with 'more'.

• Prefer / Preferable takes to (I prefer coffee to tea).

👤 Two Qualities of Same Person
Special Rule
When comparing two different qualities of the SAME person, use `more + positive degree`, NEVER '-er'!
✔ He is more wise than clever.
✖ He is wiser than clever.
✔ This hall is more wide than high.
👥 One vs Group Comparison ('Other' Rule)
Exclusion Rule
In Comparative/Positive, use 'other' with any, no, all, most to exclude the subject from its own group:
✔ Rohit is better than any other player of Indian team.
✔ Ganga is holier than any other river of India.
✔ Ganga is holier than any river of China (Ganga is not part of China!).
🚫 Non-Gradable (Absolute) Adjectives
No More / Most

Cannot be used in Comparative or Superlative degrees:

Unique, Perfect, Ideal, Supreme, Excellent, Universal, Round, Square, Triangular, Parallel, Circular, Eternal, Everlasting, Extreme, Complete, Entire, Final, Dead, Chief, Impossible, Prime.
✖ This is the most unique problem.
✔ This is a unique problem.
🗂️ Order of Adjectives (OSASCOMP)
Sequence Formula
Determiner → Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Colour → Origin → Material → Purpose → NOUN
✔ She purchased an expensive (opinion) large (size) antique (age) round (shape) yellow (colour) Italian (origin) wooden (material) dining (purpose) table.

⚡ Stage B: Active Retrieval & Fill-in Drills

Active Recall
Rule for comparing two qualities of the same person?
Use more + positive degree (e.g. He is more wise than clever, never wiser than clever).
Why is 'She is senior than me' incorrect?
Latin adjectives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior) take preposition to, not than. Correct: She is senior to me.
Difference between 'Further' and 'Farther'?
Farther relates to physical distance. Further means 'additional' or 'in addition to'.
What is wrong with 'This is the most unique monument'?
Unique is a non-gradable (absolute) adjective. It cannot take more or most. Correct: This is a unique monument.
Rule for 'worth seeing' and 'worth reading'?
They are used post-nominally (after the noun). Say a movie worth seeing, NOT a worth seeing movie.
Can we use comparative degree with 'relatively' or 'comparatively'?
No! Always use Positive Degree (e.g. Jaipur is relatively warm, not warmer).

🚀 Stage E: Adjective Quick Revision Cheat Sheets

Key error patterns that repeatedly appear in SSC and government exams.

Cheat Sheet
1. Double Degree Trap

Never use more wiser, most sweetest, more taller. Use only one comparative/superlative marker.

2. Order with 'Of the Two'

Use the + comparative when selecting between two: Ramesh is the taller of the two brothers.

3. Parallel Increase

The higher you go, the cooler it becomes. Both sides must use the + comparative.

4. Elder vs Older

Family blood relations = Elder (no 'than'). General age / things = Older than.
