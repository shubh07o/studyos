💡 What is a Noun?

"Name is Noun. Or, Everything is Noun."

1. Concrete Noun

It has Physical Appearance (can be seen & touched).

Examples: Bus, Car, Jeep, Tree, House.

2. Abstract Noun

It has No Physical Appearance (can only be felt).

Examples: Sadness, Bravery, Happiness, Gallantry, Honesty.

Types of Noun According to Property (5):

1. Proper Noun 2. Common Noun 3. Collective Noun 4. Material Noun 5. Abstract Noun

Types of Noun According to Counting (2):

1. Countable Noun 2. Uncountable Noun

🏛️ Proper Noun vs. Common Noun (Page 3)

Proper Noun

  • Individual Names: Ex: Urfi Javed, Rakhi Savant, Kangana Ranaut, Yamuna, Himalaya.
  • Capitalization Rule: First letter of any proper noun should be CAPITAL.
    • ritika → Ritika | rohan → Rohan
    • seven stages of life → Seven Stages of Life
    • madan mohan malveeya → Madan Mohan Malviya
    • united states of america → United States of America
  • All Titles are Proper Nouns.
  • Proper Adjective: Indian, American, etc. Just like Proper Nouns, the first letter of a Proper Adjective must always be capitalized.

Common Noun & Conversion Rule

A Noun which is common for same kind of things, people.
Ex: Actor, Actress, Girl, River, Teacher, House.

Rule: We can use a Proper Noun as a Common Noun! (When comparing qualities, use an article a/an/the before it):

  1. ✗ No Article Sachin is a great player. (Proper Noun)
  2. We can see a Sachin in every player of Indian team. (Common Noun)
  3. ✗ No Article Bhagat Singh is a great warrior. (Proper Noun)
  4. We can find the Bhagat Singh in every Indian soldier. (Common Noun)

Collective Noun: It refers to a group of common nouns of the same kind.

Fleet: Ships / Vehicles Flotilla: Boats Crew: Sailors Galaxy: Stars Constellation: Stars (fixed shape) Army: Soldiers Battalion: Soldiers Troop: Soldiers Infantry: Soldiers on foot Cavalry: Soldiers on horse Pack: Playing Cards / Dogs / Wolves Pride: Lions Band: Musicians Choir: Singers Orchestra: Singers / Dancers Troupe: Dancers / Artists Bunting: Small Flags Muster: Peacocks String: Camels School: Learners / Fish Bunch: Keys / Grapes Herd: Cattle / Elephants Flight: Birds Syndicate: Merchants Caravan: Gypsies / Travellers Parliament: Owls Battery: Heavy Guns Shoal: Fish Series: Events / Lectures Gang: Robbers Congregation: Worshippers Swarm: Ants / Bees Hive: Bees Kennel: Dogs Posse: Constables Staff: Members / Teachers Committee: Members Bench: A group of Judges Jury: Judges

⚖️ Collective Noun Grammar Rules (Page 5)

  1. Gentry, Infantry, Cavalry, Cattle, Poultry, Peasantry, Police: Usually plural in sense → Plural verb is used (and never add -s/-ies like gentries).
  2. United vs. Divided Rule (Jury, Committee, Team, Audience):
    • When the group is united / unanimous / acting as one unit → use a Singular Verb & Singular Pronoun (its).
    • When the members are divided / acting individually → use a Plural Verb & Plural Pronoun (their).
  3. "A [Collective] of [Plural Noun]" Rule: When constructions like "A pack of", "A gang of", "A herd of", "A pride of" are used, the verb agrees with the first/main singular term (e.g., pack, herd, pride), so a Singular Verb (is/was) is used.

2. Material Noun & Abstract Noun

Pages 5, 6 & 7 • Articles with Material Nouns, Singular-to-Plural Meaning Shifts, and Abstract Noun Suffixes

🧱 Material Noun Concepts & Rules (Pages 5–6)

Properties of Material Noun

  • Metal & Non-Metal:
    • Solid: Iron, Gold, Silver
    • Liquid: Water, Oil, Milk
    • Gas: Oxygen, Nitrogen
  • It has physical existence, can be seen, and can be touched.
  • Material noun is an Uncountable Noun.
  • Generally, we cannot make plural of a material noun (e.g., A water ✗, Some waters ✗).

Article Rule with Material Nouns

1. Generally, NO article is used before a Material Noun.
Ex: (No Article) Water is colourless.
Ex: (No Article) Milk is sweet.
Ex: (No Article) Gold is more precious than (No Article) Iron.

2. Exception (Specified by Phrase/Clause): When a phrase or clause is added to specify the Material Noun, "The" is used before it!
Ex: The water of this river is not polluted.
Ex: The milk of cow is sweet.
Ex: The gold of this ring is pure.

3. Special Cases (Page 6):
• "All that glitters is not gold." (Here gold = Abstract quality ✓)
• "India has won many golds." (Here golds = Gold medals ✓)
• "There are five waters in all over the world." (Meaning changed to Oceans ✓)

💭 Abstract Noun Concepts & Suffixes (Pages 6–7)

What is an Abstract Noun?

  • Includes: Emotions, State of mind, System, Belief, Action.
  • An Abstract noun can be felt only; it does not have physical existence.
  • Abstract nouns are Uncountable Nouns (Singular verb is used; never pluralize like sympathies, loyalties, gratitudes).
  • Examples (Not just feelings!): Sadness, Bravery, Gallantry, Happiness, Polygamy, Atheism, Democracy, Dance, Childhood, Birth, Growth, Enjoyment, Ambition, Accuracy.

Identification via Suffixes

-ness (Sadness, Happiness) -gamy (Polygamy) -th (Birth, Growth) -tion (Ambition) -hood (Childhood) -ence / -ance -cracy (Democracy) -ment (Enjoyment) -logy -y (Bravery, Accuracy) -ism (Atheism)

Note: Words ending in -ist or -er denote people (Common Nouns).

🌟 Making Abstract Noun from Common Noun (Page 7): Use "The + Common Noun + in him/her"

When referring to the inner quality/feeling inside a person, place "The" before the Common Noun:

  • Mother → The mother in her | Sister → The sister in her | Brother → The brother in him | Father → The father in him | Player → The player in him
  • 1. [The] Mother in her could not see the child burning live.
  • 2. Generally, brother and sister quarrel with each other but on the day of Raksha Bandhan, [the] sister in her wakes up.
  • 3. When he sees Virat's batting, [the] player in him wakes up.

3. Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

Pages 7, 8 & 9 • Master List of Uncountable Nouns, Noun vs. Verb Pairs, and 29 Exam Questions

⚖️ Countable vs. Uncountable Rules (Page 7)

Countable Nouns

  • Nouns which can be counted (have Singular & Plural forms).
  • Can use A / An.
  • Use Many and Few.
  • Examples from Page 7: Pen, Boy, Horse, Star, Six Boys, Shop, Machine, Poem (Sonnet, Elegy, Satire, Epic, Ode), Bench/Sofa/Bed/Chair, Rupee/Dollar.

Uncountable Nouns

  • Nouns which cannot be counted (Always Singular; Cannot be made Plural with -s/-es).
  • Do NOT use A / An directly before them! Instead, use: A piece of, An item of, An act of, A slice of, etc.
  • Use Much and Little.
  • Note on Measurements: Milk (Uncountable), 5 Litre Milk (Still Uncountable!), Sugar (Uncountable), 5 kg Sugar (Uncountable), Soap (Uncountable).

📌 High-Frequency Uncountable Nouns in SSC Exams (Page 7)

Machinery (Countable unit: machine) Work Advice Information Stationery (pen, pencil) Luggage Baggage Cookery Knowledge News (Singular! vs. Tidings = Plural) Mischief Clothing / Wear (pants, jeans) Traffic (car, bus, bike) Evidence (knife, gun, letter) Equipment (tool) Jewellery Feedback Revenue Violence Poetry (Sonnet, elegy, satire, epic, ode) Furniture (bench, sofa, bed, chair) Money (rupee, dollar) Scenery (river, mountain, people) Bread / Ice / Cake / Time

⚠️ Confusing Noun vs. Verb Pairs (Page 7)

• Advice (Noun) → सलाह
• Advise (Verb) → सलाह देना
• Practice (Noun) → अभ्यास
• Practise (Verb) → अभ्यास करना
• Effect (Noun) → प्रभाव
• Affect (Verb) → प्रभाव डालना

4. Dozen/Hundred, Compound Nouns & Foreign Plurals

Pages 9, 10, 11 & 12 • Unit Rules, Compound Noun Plurals/Possessives, and Latin/Greek Singular-Plural Tables

🔢 Concept of Dozen / Hundred / Thousand / Lakh / Million / Score (Page 9)

Rule 1: Preceded by a Definite Number → SINGULAR

Number + Dozen / Hundred / Thousand / Lakh / Million + Plural Noun (No -s, No "of")

  • Two dozen eggs (दो दर्जन अंडे)
  • Two dozen books
  • Five thousand students / people (पाँच हजार छात्र)
  • Two lakh copies
  • Two score books

Rule 2: No Number + Followed by "of" → PLURAL

Dozens / Hundreds / Thousands / Millions + of + Plural Noun

  • Dozens of eggs / apples (दर्जनों अंडे)
  • Thousands of students (हजारों छात्र)
  • Hundreds of applicants

🔗 Concept of Compound Noun (Pages 10–11)

Definition: The noun which is made of more than one word. Ex: Commander-in-chief, Woman servant.

4 Types of Compound Nouns & Their Plurals

  1. Type 1 (Noun + Noun): Pluralize the 2nd (main) noun.
    • Railway station → Railway stations | Bus terminal → Bus terminals
    • Step daughter → Step daughters | Step son → Step sons | Step mother → Step mothers | Maid servant → Maid servants | Pick pocket → Pick pockets
  2. Type 2 (Noun + Preposition/Adverb): Pluralize the 1st (noun) part.
    • Passer-by → Passers-by | Looker-on → Lookers-on | Hanger-on → Hangers-on | Looker-out → Lookers-out
  3. Type 3 (Noun + Preposition + Noun): Pluralize the 1st (head) noun.
    • Brother-in-law → Brothers-in-law | Daughter-in-law → Daughters-in-law | Commander-in-chief → Commanders-in-chief | Good-for-nothing → Goods-for-nothing
  4. Type 4 (Noun + Preposition + Same Noun): NO PLURAL! Always Singular Noun + Singular Verb!
    • Door to door | Step by step | City after city | Town after town | Page after page

Special Compound Plural & Possessive Rules

  • Profession vs. Gender (Man/Woman):
    • Washer man → Washer men | Post man → Post men | Dutch man → Dutch men | Kins man → Kins men | French man → French men
    • Rule: If a compound noun is formed with man or woman to denote gender/profession of the whole person, BOTH become plural:
    • Man servant → Men servants
    • Woman servant → Women servants
  • "-ful" Nouns (Add -s at the very end):
    • Handful → Handfuls | Mouthful → Mouthfuls | Spoonful → Spoonfuls | Glassful/Grassful → Glassfuls/Grassfuls | Cupful → Cupfuls
  • Possessive of Compound Noun: Add 's at the end of the entire word/phrase:
    • Brother-in-law's house | Commander-in-chief's orders | Brothers-in-law's wallet.

5. Five Special Categories of Nouns (Pages 12–14)

Subjects ending in -ics, Paired Tools, Always-Plural Nouns, Collective Plurals, Titles, Innings, Summons & Wages

🗂️ The 5 Special Types of Nouns (Pages 12–13)

TYPE 1: Looks Plural (-s), But is SINGULAR

  • Subjects: Physics, Civics, Economics, Mathematics, Phonetics, Statistics, Linguistics, Dynamics.
  • Games: Gymnastics, Billiards, Draughts.
  • Diseases: Measles, Mumps, Rickets, Diabetes.
  • ⚠️ Exception (Possessive / Calculation):
    • Subject → Singular (Mathematics is a difficult subject.)
    • Preceded by Possessive (My/His/The + Noun referring to calculation/data) → Plural! (His mathematics are not good. The statistics of this company are not reviewed.)

TYPE 2: Paired Nouns (Always PLURAL)

Scissors, Trousers, Binoculars, Spectacles, Goggles, Pincers, Tongs, Pants, Jeans, Breeches, Slippers, Shoes.

  • These nouns have two parts joined together → Always take a Plural Verb.
  • Never remove -s (A trouser ✗, A spectacle ✗) and never use a/an directly (A trousers ✗).
  • To make singular/countable, use "A pair of...":
    • A pair of trousers was bought.
    • Two pairs of trousers were bought.

TYPE 3: Nouns Always PLURAL in Form & Verb

Rations, Ashes, Gallows, Surroundings, Thanks, Regards, Auspices, Proceedings, Contents, Riches, Credentials, Assets, Tidings, Alms, Congratulations, Amends, Savings, Earnings, Dues, Ruins, Odds, Valuables, Movables, Embers, Fetters, Arrears, Outskirts, Winnings, Orders, Goods, Belongings, Bowels.

  • Content (adj.) = satisfied vs. Contents (N) = विषय-वस्तु.
  • Riches = assets/wealth (Proverb: "Riches have wings").
  • Order (V) vs. Orders (N) = official instructions.

TYPE 4: Looks Singular, But Always PLURAL

Clergy, Peasantry, Cattle, People, Police, Electorate, Poultry, Gentry, Vermin.

  • Never add -s (Cattles ✗).
  • Four cattle ✓ OR Four heads of cattle ✓.
  • People vs. Peoples: People = लोग (Plural) | Peoples = Nations / Races (विभिन्न राष्ट्रों के लोग).

TYPE 5: Titles & Country Names (Always SINGULAR)

Seven Stages of Life, Daffodils, Arabian/Amazing Nights, Gulliver's Travels, The Canterbury Tales, The Three Musketeers, United States of America, The Netherlands. → Always take a Singular Verb (is/was).

Special Cases: Innings, Summons & Wages (Page 13)

  • INNINGS: Never "Inning ✗". Always spelled Innings.
    • First innings was... | Fourth innings was... | Four innings were...
  • SUMMONS:
    • Summon = Verb | Summons = Singular Noun (was) | Summonses = Plural Noun (were/have).
  • WAGES:
    • Labour money = Plural (His wages were looted.)
    • Result/Outcome = Singular (The wages of sin is death.)

6. Concept of 'Of', Fractions, Modifiers & Same-Form Nouns

Pages 14, 15 & 16 • One of the + Plural Noun, Fraction Agreement, Noun Modifiers, and Invariable Nouns

🎯 Concept of Preposition "OF" & Fractions (Pages 14–15)

"One of / Each of / Either of" Rule

One / Two / Each / Either / Neither / None / Most / Many + OF + The/Determiner + PLURAL NOUN/PRONOUN

  • After "one of", the first countable noun that follows is ALWAYS PLURAL, and "the" (or another determiner like these/my) is required before it.
  • 1. The advice of my friend / friends (Both ✓)
  • 2. One of the students (has done it)
  • 3. Two of the workers
  • 4. Each of the movies
  • 5. Everyone of them

Concept of Fraction (Page 15)

With A part of / One third of / 50% of / Most of / Part of, the verb depends on the noun after "of":

  • Singular / Uncountable Noun → Singular Verb
    • One third of the movie was interesting.
    • One part of the mango is rotten.
    • 20% of the milk was bought.
  • Plural Countable Noun → Plural Verb
    • One third of the movies were interesting.
    • 20% of the books were very useful.

⚡ "One of" & Fraction Questions (Pages 14–15)

📏 Concept of Modifier & Invariable Nouns (Pages 15–16)

Noun as a Modifier (Adjective) → ALWAYS SINGULAR!

Article/Number + [Number + Singular Modifier Noun] + Main Noun

When a noun comes before another noun to describe/modify it, the modifier noun functions as an adjective and cannot be pluralized:

  • A five hundred rupee note (NOT hundreds or rupees)
  • Two five hundred rupee notes (Main noun notes is plural!)
  • A five kilometer race
  • A five man committee | A four boy union
  • Delicate eye surgery | Five two-hundred-page books

Nouns Identical in Singular & Plural (Pages 15–16)

Fish, sheep, deer, vocation, offspring, species, headquarters, aircraft, spacecraft, wages, means, fruit.

  • Fish vs. Fishes / Fruit vs. Fruits: Use singular form for food/general plural (We take fruit after meal), but use Fishes / Fruits when referring to different species, kinds, or varieties (There are many kinds of fishes/fruits).
  • Headquarters / Means / Species: Take singular or plural verb depending on whether one or multiple are meant.

7. Cases of Noun & Possessive Case ('s) Rules

Pages 16, 17 & 18 • 5 Cases, Living vs. Non-Living Possession, Double Possessive, and Common vs. Split Possession

👑 The 5 Cases of Noun (Page 16)

1. Subjective (Nominative)

Rohan plays well. (Acts as Subject)

2. Objective (Accusative)

I helped Rohan. (Acts as Object)

3. Possessive Case ('s / of)

Rohan's friend. (Shows ownership)

4. Vocative Case (S, V1)

"Rohan, come here."
• He said, "Boys, work hard" → He advised boys to work hard.
• He said, "Boys work hard." → He said that boys worked hard.

5. Appositive Case (S, ___, V)

Rohan, my friend, is working hard.
Rule: Verb agrees with the first main subject, NOT the appositive phrase!

🔑 Complete Rules of Possessive Case ('s vs. of) (Pages 16–18)

1. Living vs. Non-Living Things

  • Living Things: Use 's or of (Ram's Bat, Rohan's Cat, Ritika's Brother).
  • Non-Living Things: "Of" is compulsory! Do NOT use 's:
    • Table's leg ✗ → The leg of the table ✓
    • Room's wall ✗ → The wall of the room ✓
    • Fan's blade ✗ → The blade of the fan ✓
  • Exceptions where Non-Living Things TAKE ('s):
    • Personification: Death's icy hands, Nature's beauty, Fortune's favourite, Heaven's mercy, Duty's call, Mind's eye.
    • Heavenly Bodies: Sun's rays, Earth's atmosphere/temperature.
    • Time / Weight / Distance / Idioms: A day's leave, A pound's weight, Today's function, Yesterday's programme, This year's campaign, At a stone's throw.
    • Countries / Cities / Organizations: Mumbai's people, Kanpur's students, India's success, Country's population, Company's report, Society's needs, Government's decision, Court's decision.

2. Plural Possessive, Double Possessive & Joint/Split

  • Plural Nouns ending in -s: Add ONLY an apostrophe (') to avoid a hissing sound:
    • Boys' Hostel ✓ (Not Boy's or Boys's)
    • Girls' Degree College ✓ | Workers' organization ✓ | Babies' faces ✓ | For justice' sake / peace' sake ✓
    • Irregular Plurals (not ending in -s) take 's: Children's Toys ✓, People's behaviour ✓, Men's / Women's ✓.
  • Sequential Double Possession is NOT Allowed (N's N's ✗):
    • My daughter's son's dog ✗ → The dog of my daughter's son ✓
    • My uncle's secretary's wife ✗ → The wife of my uncle's secretary ✓
  • Indefinite Pronouns + "Else":
    • Somebody's / Everybody's problem ✓
    • With else, put 's on else: Somebody's else ✗ → Somebody else's ✓.
  • Common (Joint) vs. Split Possession (Page 18):
    • Common Possession (Shared item): Noun 1 and Noun 2's → India and Pakistan's border; Rohan and Rahul's joint account; Sandeep and Vivek's friendship; Ram and Sita's house.
    • Split Possession (Separate items): Noun 1's and Noun 2's → India's and Pakistan's players; Rohan's and Rahul's accounts; Dinkar's and Nirala's poetry.

8. Confusing Nouns & Superfluous Expressions

Pages 18, 19 & 20 • Word Distinctions, Redundant Phrases, and Parts of Speech Conversions

🔍 Confusing Noun Pairs (Pages 18–20)

  • Astronomer vs. Astrologer: Astronomer = Scientist of celestial bodies | Astrologer = Fortune teller (predicts future).
  • Place vs. Room: Place = General location | Room = Unoccupied space for a specific purpose (in a car, bench, class, compartment).
  • Center vs. Middle: Center = Central point of a circle/building | Middle = Between two sides (e.g., middle of the road).
  • Habit vs. Custom vs. Customs: Habit = Individual tendency | Custom = Social tradition | Customs = Border taxes.
  • Female vs. Woman: Female = Gender/sex classification | Woman = Adult female human being (I saw two women in the park).
  • Envy vs. Jealousy: Envy = Wanting what another person has | Jealousy = Fear of losing something/someone you already have to another person.
  • Talent vs. Skill: Talent = Natural ability (by birth) | Skill = Ability developed through learning or practice (e.g., driving).
  • Ground vs. Floor vs. Grounds: Ground = Surface of the earth (outdoors) | Floor = Surface of a room indoors | Grounds = Valid reasons or justification.
  • Sign vs. Signature: Sign = Symbol/indication (Noun) or Verb | Signature = A person's written name (Noun).
  • Brick and Stone: Uncountable when used as building materials (made of brick and stone); Countable when referring to separate bricks/stones.
  • Word vs. Words: Word (C.N.) = शब्द | Word (U.N. Singular) = Promise (Man of word; true to one's word).
  • Alphabet vs. Alphabets: Alphabet = वर्णमाला (A–Z set of letters) | Alphabets = Languages.
  • Pain vs. Pains: Pain = Suffering | Pains = Hard work / great effort (takes pains over his work).
  • House vs. Home: House = Building (buy a new house) | Home = Emotional attachment / residence (Go home = Adverb; Go to his home = Noun).
