🌐 Indian Federalism: Three-Tier Structure & Canadian Model

Federalism refers to the existence of more than one level of government. States are not merely agents of the Centre; they derive their constitutional authority directly from the Constitution itself.

🏛️ Tier 1
Union / Central Government

National affairs, defence, foreign relations

🏢 Tier 2
State Governments

State-level governance, police, agriculture

🏡 Tier 3
Local Bodies

Panchayats (Rural) & Municipalities (Urban)

🍁 Borrowed from Canada: India is described as a "Federation with a unitary bias" or a "Federation with a Strong Centre".

🏛️ Parliamentary System & Organs of Government

India adopted the Parliamentary Form of Government (Westminster model) borrowed from the UK.

⚠️ Crucial Nuance:
• In India (Parliamentary): There is NO strict separation of power. The Executive is drawn from the Legislative branch (Ministers are MPs).
• In USA (Presidential): There is a strict separation of powers between Executive, Legislature, and Judiciary.

🏞️ Schedule 5: Scheduled Areas (10 States)

  • Constitutional Basis: Article 244(1). The President of India has the sole authority to declare areas as "Scheduled Areas".
  • Tribal Advisory Council (TAC): Can have up to 20 members, of which 3/4th must be representatives of Scheduled Tribes in the State Legislative Assembly (ST MLAs).
  • Role of TAC: Exclusively advises the Governor of the respective state.
📍 10 States with 5th Schedule Areas:
Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Telangana.

🌲 Schedule 6: Autonomous Districts (4 States)

🧠 MEMORY TRICK: "ATM without Money"
Assam, Tripura, Meghalaya, Mizoram.
⚠️ Remember: Manipur is NOT included!
  • Autonomous District Councils (ADCs): Constituted for 5 years by the Governor.
  • Powers of ADCs: Vested with Legislative, Judicial, Financial, and Executive powers to manage land, forests, and village administration.
  • Council Membership: Up to 30 members (26 elected by adult suffrage + 4 nominated by Governor).
  • Special Exception: Bodo Territorial Council (BTC) consists of 46 members (40 elected + 6 nominated by Governor).

📑 Schedule 7: Legislative Power Distribution & State List Overrides

1. Union List

Initially 97 ➔ Presently 100

Defence, Arms & ammunition, Atomic energy, Citizenship, Airways, Inter-state trade, Banking, Foreign affairs, Labour & mines, Census.

2. State List

Initially 66 ➔ Presently 61

Public order, Local government, Health & sanitation, Pilgrimages, Intoxicating liquors, Agriculture, Fisheries, Betting/gambling, Co-operative societies, Land, Lotteries.

3. Concurrent List

Initially 47 ➔ Presently 52

Marriage & divorce, Criminal laws & CrPC, Insolvency & bankruptcy, Trade & labour disputes, Electricity, Education, Weights & measures.

⚡ Doctrine of Repugnancy [Article 254(1)]:
If a conflict arises between Central law and State law on a Concurrent List item, Central law prevails.
Exception: A State law prevails only if it was reserved for the President's consideration and received President's Assent. (However, Parliament can still override it later with a new enactment!).
🌟 Residuary Powers: Any matter not enumerated in the three lists belongs exclusively to the Parliament (borrowed from Canada).

🔄 42nd CAA, 1976: 5 Transferred Subjects

MOVED FROM STATE LIST ➔ CONCURRENT LIST
  1. Education (including university, technical, and vocational education)
  2. Forests
  3. Weights and Measures
  4. Protection of Wild Animals and Birds
  5. Administration of Justice (constitution and organization of all courts except the Supreme Court and High Courts)
This historic transfer reduced the State List from 66 to 61 and increased the Concurrent List from 47 to 52 subjects.

📜 Schedules 9, 10, 11, 12 & Key Judicial Rulings

Schedule 9: Land Reforms (1st CAA, 1951)

Introduced to protect agrarian land reform and abolition of Zamindari laws from fundamental rights challenge in courts.

⚖️ I.R. Coelho Case (2007) Landmark Ruling:
The Supreme Court held that laws placed in the 9th Schedule after April 24, 1973 (the date of Kesavananda Bharati judgment) are open to Judicial Review if they violate the Basic Structure of the Constitution!

Schedule 10: Anti-Defection Law (52nd CAA, 1985)

Provides for disqualification of MPs and MLAs on grounds of political defection. Enacted under Rajiv Gandhi government.

Schedule 11: Panchayats (73rd CAA, 1992)

  • Added by 73rd Amendment Act, 1992.
  • Contains 29 functional matters (e.g., agriculture, drinking water, minor irrigation).
  • Part IX added to the Constitution.

Schedule 12: Municipalities (74th CAA, 1992)

  • Added by 74th Amendment Act, 1992.
  • Contains 18 functional matters (e.g., urban planning, roads, public health).
  • Part IX-A added to the Constitution.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK: 29 vs 18 Functional Matters
• For Panchayats: 2 + 9 = 11 (Matches 11th Schedule!)
• For Municipalities: 18 matters (18 + 11 = 29!).

⚡ Commonly Confused Concepts

5th Schedule vs 6th Schedule

5th Schedule: Administration of Scheduled Areas in 10 States. Governed with Tribal Advisory Council (up to 20 members, 3/4th ST MLAs).
6th Schedule: Administration of Tribal Areas in ONLY 4 States (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram) with Autonomous District Councils (ADCs).

Article 249 vs Article 250

Article 249: Rajya Sabha resolution in National Interest (valid for 1 year).
Article 250: Parliament legislating during a National Emergency (valid until 6 months after emergency ceases).

Integrated Judiciary vs Independent Judiciary

Integrated Judiciary (Unitary): A single pyramidal hierarchy of courts enforcing both central and state laws.
Independent Judiciary (Federal): Judiciary is free from executive and legislative interference to protect the Constitution and federal balance.

Schedule 2 vs Schedule 3

Schedule 2 (E - Emoluments): Salaries and allowances (President, Judges, CAG, etc.).
Schedule 3 (A - Affirmations): Oaths and affirmations forms (Ministers, MPs, Judges, CAG).
