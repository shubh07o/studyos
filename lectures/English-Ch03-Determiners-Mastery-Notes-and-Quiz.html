Determiners — Digital Notes & 100 PYQ Quiz

1. Determiners vs. Adjectives
Foundation
Determiner: Determines/points out a noun. Always placed before a noun (Attributive only).
Adjective: Adds quality/meaning to a noun. Can be Attributive or Predicative.
FeatureDeterminerAdjective
Gradable (Degrees)?No (Non-gradable)*Yes (wise, wiser, wisest)
PositionOnly Attributive (Before Noun)Attributive & Predicative
Articles NoteAll Articles (A, An, The) are Determiners, but not all Determiners are Articles.
  • ✓ Some soldiers are brave. ("Some" = Determiner [Attributive], "brave" = Adjective [Predicative])
  • ✓ I met a brave soldier. ("a" = Determiner, "brave" = Attributive Adj.)
2. Group 'A' vs. Group 'B' Rule
High Yield
Golden Rule: Never use two Group A determiners consecutively! You can only use one Group A determiner at a time, or combine with Group B / "of".
Group 'A' (Never stack together)Group 'B' (Can combine)
1. Articles: A, An, The
2. Possessives: My, Our, Your, His, Her, Its, Their
3. Demonstratives: This, That, These, Those 		1. Quantifiers: Some, any, few, little, much, many
2. Ordinals/Pre: First, second, all, both (e.g. many a, a few, a little, all these, all our)
  • ✗ I have found a your this bag.
  • ✓ I have found a / your / this bag.
  • ✗ You are my the best friend. → ✓ You are my best friend. (No article)
  • ✗ I searched in every my cupboard. → ✓ ...in every cupboard of mine.
3. Demonstratives (This/That/These/Those)
Top PYQ Trap
Number agreement must match across Demonstrative + Kind/Sort/Type + Noun:
• This / That + singular noun (kind / sort / type / product)
• These / Those + plural noun (kinds / sorts / types / products)
  • ✗ Those kind of conclusions / These sort of arguments
  • ✓ Those kinds of conclusions / These sorts of arguments
  • ✗ That kinds of objections / This products
  • ✓ That kind of objection (or Those kinds) / This product (or These products)
4. Each vs. Every
Distributive
Both take a Singular Countable Noun and a Singular Verb when used as subject determiners.
FeatureEachEvery
Part of SpeechDeterminer & PronounOnly Determiner (Never Pronoun)
NumberFor 2 or more than 2 (Individual focus)Only for more than 2 (+2) (General group)
With "of"✓ Each of the boys is...✗ Every of the boys (Always Wrong!)
Special Triggers"They each + Plural Verb" (We each have...)Used after Almost, Nearly, Practically, Approximately & Time Repetition (Every Sunday, Every hour)
  • ✗ Every of the two girls will get the reward. → ✓ Each of the two girls...
  • ✗ We each has participated. → ✓ We each have participated. (Verb agrees with real plural subject 'We')
  • ✓ Every word and every line fascinates us. (Singular verb + 'his/her' pronoun)
5. Either / Neither vs. Any / None
Distributive
• Either / Neither are strictly for 2 persons or things and take a Singular Verb.
• For more than 2 (+2): replace Either with Any, and Neither with None.
  • Structure 1: Either/Neither + Singular Noun + Singular Verb (Neither boy is ready.)
  • Structure 2: Either/Neither + of + Plural Noun + Singular Verb (Either of these two roads leads...)
  • ✗ Shailja is prettier than either of her three cousins.
  • ✓ Shailja is prettier than any of her three cousins. (3 cousins = +2)
6. Some vs. Any
Quantifier
RuleSomeAny
Sentence TypeAffirmative sentencesNegative & Interrogative sentences
Nouns AllowedUncountable Noun OR Plural Countable Noun (Some advice, Some boys — ✗ Some boy)Uncountable, Singular Countable, or Plural Countable (Any book, Any books, Any milk)
Interrogative ExceptionsUse Some in Questions for Offers, Requests (Would/Can/Could you...) or when expected answer is Yes.Use Any after Negative words: Hardly, Scarcely, Rarely, Barely, Seldom, Without, Forbade, Refuse, Deny, Doubt.
  • ✗ Would you like to have any biscuits with tea? → ✓ ...some biscuits with tea? (Offer)
  • ✗ He climbed with hardly no / some effort. → ✓ ...hardly any effort.
  • ✓ In affirmative meaning "All/Whichever": "I can solve any question."
7. Few vs. A Few vs. The Few
Countable Plural
Always used with Plural Countable Nouns (C.N.). Never use with Uncountable nouns (like water, money, rice, time, juice).
FormMeaningHindi Sense / Trigger
FewHardly any / Almost nothing (Negative)ना के बराबर (काम न चल सके) • Trigger: Very few
A fewSome / A small number (Positive)कुछ (काम चल सके) • Trigger: Only a few, a few decades ago
The fewNot many, but all that is availableजितना है वह सारा • Trigger: Followed by a Relative Clause (that/who + S + V)
  • ✗ Until few decades ago / In few words, he expressed gratitude.
  • ✓ Until a few decades ago / In a few words...
  • ✓ I have given you the few books that I had. (Relative clause present)
8. Little vs. A Little vs. The Little
Uncountable Singular
Always used with Uncountable Singular Nouns (U.N.) (money, water, time, patience, knowledge, rice, juice).
FormMeaningTrigger / Notes
LittleHardly any / Almost nothing (-ve)ना के बराबर • Trigger: Very little (✗ Never say "a very little"!)
A littleSome quantity (+ve)थोड़ा/कुछ • Trigger: Only a little (A little knowledge is dangerous)
The littleAll that small amount availableFollowed by a Relative Clause (The little money that I had...)
  • ✗ There is a very little time to prepare. → ✓ There is very little time... (Drop 'a')
  • ✗ Ritwik set aside a few money / a few rice. → ✓ ...a little money / a little rice.
9. Many / Much / Many a / A lot of
Quantifier
• Many (Fewer / Fewest): Plural Countable Nouns (flaws, motifs, applications, mistakes, books).
• Much (Less / Least): Uncountable Singular Nouns (effort, thought, time, work, water).
  • Many a / an + Singular Noun + Singular Verb: ✓ Many an hour / Many an opportunity / Many a musician has...
  • A great many + Plural Noun + Plural Verb: ✓ A great many workers are...
  • A lot of / Lots of: Both take Plural C.N. or Singular U.N. ✗ A lots of / Lot of people → ✓ A lot of / Lots of people
  • Much too vs Too much: Too much + Noun (Too much technology) vs Much too + Adjective (Much too painful).
  • No fewer than vs No less than: For countable plural students: ✓ No fewer than fifty students were absent.
10. All vs. Both vs. Whole
Word Order
WordPosition of "The" / PossessiveUsage Rule
AllAfter All (All the books, All our teachers, All these novels)For +2 Countable (Pl. Verb) or Uncountable (Sing. Verb: All the milk has been sold). Never use All + a/an.
BothAfter Both (Both the students, Both my children)Strictly for 2. Not used in negative sentences! Use Neither of the... is instead of Both... are not.
WholeBefore Whole (The whole country, The whole chapter)Used with singular countable noun to denote complete unit.
  • ✗ The both children / Whole country applauded.
  • ✓ Both the children / The whole country applauded.
11. Ordinals vs. Cardinals & "Enough"
Sequence
• Ordinals (Position): First, second, third, next, last, previous → Always take "The" and come BEFORE Cardinals (one, two, three, few).
• Formula: The + Ordinal + Cardinal + Noun
  • ✗ The five first girls / The few next days / Fashion might not be first thing
  • ✓ The first five girls / The next few days / ...be the first thing
  • Enough Position: Enough + Noun (✓ enough experience, ✗ experience enough) vs Adjective + Enough (brave enough).
  • Too + Adj + A/An + Noun: ✗ too grave sin → ✓ too grave a sin
12. Other / Another & Quantity Phrases
Agreement
ExpressionNoun & Verb Rule
Another (An + Other)Takes Singular Noun (another cup, another outfit). Never use "The another" or "His another" (use His other brother). Idiom: "In other words" (✗ In another words).
The otherSpecific remaining one of two (One is red, the other is blue).
OthersPronoun only (No noun after it: Others can never understand).
A number of / A large number ofPlural Countable Noun + Plural Verb (A number of cars are...)
The number ofPlural Countable Noun + Singular Verb (The number of players is...)
