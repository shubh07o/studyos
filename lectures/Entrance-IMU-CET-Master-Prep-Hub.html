What Kind of Vocabulary & English Topics Does IMU CET Ask?

Based on a forensic linguistic analysis of all 37 uploaded papers (IMU CET 2021–2026, Official Sample Papers, Seamate, and Agniveer Vayu), IMU CET does not ask random dictionary words. Instead, 90% of questions strictly fall into 5 High-Probability Clusters and 6 Core Grammar Pillars.

Part A: The 5 Vocabulary Archetypes Asked in IMU CET

Archetype #1 • 28% Weight

Officer Personality & Character Adjectives

Because Merchant Navy officers require composure, discipline, and leadership under pressure, IMU CET heavily tests adjectives describing human temperament, judgment, and speech habits.

Must-Know Asked Words:

Unwavering, Stoic, Astute, Judicious, Fastidious, Decorous, Serene,Resilience, Loquacious ↔ Taciturn, Benevolent ↔ Malevolent, Capricious, Unscrupulous, Ascetic.

Archetype #2 • 18% Weight

Maritime, Navigation, Weather & Action Idioms

Being a maritime university exam, paper setters frequently pick idioms and words tied to storms, sea navigation, wreckage, motion, and crisis survival.

Must-Know Asked Words:

To weather the storm, To be in the doldrums (equatorial calm sea zone), To fish in troubled waters, Thalassophobia (fear of sea), Debris (wreckage), Propel, Circuitous, Stationary, Averted, Hazardous.

Archetype #3 • 22% Weight

Greek/Latin Root One-Word Substitutions

One-Word Substitutions in IMU CET 2024–2026 heavily rely on classic prefixes like Poly-, Omni-, In-/Ir- (negation), and suffixes like -cracy, -logy, and -phobia.

Must-Know Asked Words:

Polymath, Polyglot, Omnipotent, Omnivores, Autocracy, Anarchist, Incorrigible, Irrevocable, Invincible, Infallible, Indelible, Inevitable, Eulogy vs Dirge.

Archetype #4 • 17% Weight

Applied Science, Material & Trade Terminology

Unique to IMU CET: English questions often test one-word terms from Physics (material properties), Biology (disease spread), and Maritime Trade/Economics.

Must-Know Asked Words:

Malleable, Ductile, Brittle, Opaque, Translucent, Catalyst, Potable vs Edible, Pandemic vs Endemic vs Contagious, Tariff, Subsidy, Monopoly, Inflation, Insolvent, Actuary.

Archetype #5 • 15% Weight

Clarity vs Ambiguity & Scale Polarity Pairs

Antonym questions repeatedly test the contrast between "Clear/Concise vs Vague/Wordy" and "Abundant/Grand vs Scarce/Humble".

Must-Know Asked Words:

Verbose ↔ Concise, Equivocal ↔ Unequivocal, Obfuscate ↔ Clarify, Vague ↔ Specific, Lavish ↔ Frugal, Grandiose ↔ Modest, Superficial ↔ Profound, Exodus ↔ Influx.

Golden Root Decoder

Decode Unseen Words in 5 Seconds

  • POLY- (Many): Polyglot (languages), Polymath (subjects)
  • OMNI- (All): Omnipotent (power), Omnivore (eater)
  • EU- (Good/Praise): Eulogy (funeral praise) vs Dirge (mournful song)
  • MAL- / BENE- (Evil / Good): Malevolent ↔ Benevolent
  • LOQU- (Speak): Loquacious (talkative) ↔ Taciturn (silent)
  • IN- / IR- (Not): Infallible, Invincible, Indelible, Incorrigible

Part B: Complete IMU CET English Must-Read Syllabus

Section Weightage based on IMU CET UG Official Structure (25–40 Questions across shifts)

1. Vocabulary (Synonyms, Antonyms, One-Word, Idioms) 35% – 40% (12–15 Qs)

What to Study: Direct & contextual synonyms/antonyms, idioms in sentences, phrasal verbs (look into, call off, put up with, give in), and one-word scientific/governance terms. Master all 150 words in Tab 1 first.

2. Spotting Errors, Tenses & Subject-Verb Agreement 20% – 25% (6–8 Qs)

Must-Read Rules: • Either/Or, Neither/Nor, Along with, As well as verb agreement.
• 3 Golden Conditionals: Type 1 (If + Present, Will + V1), Type 2 (If + Were/V2, Would + V1), Type 3 (If + Had + V3, Would have + V3 — asked every single year!).
• Confusing pairs: Since vs For, Beside vs Besides, Few vs A few, Hardly/Scarcely...when, No sooner...than.

3. Fixed Prepositions, Articles & Determiners 15% (4–5 Qs)

High-Repeat Prepositions: Senior/Junior/Prefer to, Accused of, Acquitted of, Congratulate on, Abide by, Cope with (never 'cope up with'), Comprise (no 'of' in active voice), Insist on, Prevent from, Good at.

4. Active/Passive Voice & Direct/Indirect Narration 12% – 15% (3–5 Qs)

What to Study: Imperative sentences (Let the door be shut), Interrogative sentences starting with Who / By whom, and tense backshifting in Indirect Speech (Present Perfect → Past Perfect, 'Tomorrow' → 'The next day').

5. Reading Comprehension, Cloze Test & Para Jumbles (PQRS) 10% – 12% (3–5 Qs)

Strategy: For PQRS sentence rearrangement, use mandatory pair elimination (pronoun links, chronological connectors like However, Therefore, Consequently).

Top 8 Golden Grammar Traps in IMU CET

1. "One of the": Always takes a Plural Noun + Singular Verb.
Ex: One of the cadets is (not are) selected.
2. Uncountable Nouns (Singular Verb, No 's'):
Luggage, Baggage, Scenery, Information, Furniture, Advice, Equipment, Machinery.
3. "Lest" Rule: Lest is always followed by should (never may/will/not).
Ex: Work hard lest you should fail.
4. "It is high time / It is time": Followed by Past Simple (V2).
Ex: It is high time we started (not start) our revision.
5. "Unless" & "Until": Both are negative; never use "not" in the same clause.
Ex: Unless you work hard (NOT unless you do not work hard)...
6. "Look forward to / Accustomed to / With a view to": Always take a Gerund (V1+ing).
Ex: I look forward to joining (not join) the ship.
