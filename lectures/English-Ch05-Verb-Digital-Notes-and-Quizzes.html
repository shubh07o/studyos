Root Etymology: The Latin Verbum ("A Word")

A verb is the backbone of language. Without a verb, a sentence cannot express a complete thought.

1. Verb Action / State

A word indicating action, state, or possession.

He is reading a novel.
2. Verbatim Exact Words

Word for word; exactly as originally spoken or written.

He quoted the passage verbatim.
3. Verbiage Excess Words

Use of too many unnecessary words; prolixity.

Cut down the verbose verbiage.
4. Verbose Style Trait

Expressed in more words than needed; wordy.

A verbose explanation confuses readers.
5. Verbal Oral / Written

Relating to or consisting of words (often spoken).

They had a verbal agreement.
6. Proverb Saying

A concise, traditional maxim stating a general truth.

"Practice makes perfect" is a proverb.

The Three Functional Dimensions of a Verb

1. Action

Tells what the subject performs (Doing) or what spontaneously takes place (Happening).

• Doing: He is reading a novel.
• Happening: The river overflowed.
2. Possession

Expresses ownership or belonging without performing a physical action.

• He has a novel. (Possession)
• She owns three vintage cars.
3. State

Links the subject to a complement defining identity, condition, or feeling.

• He is a novelist. (Identity)
• She seems happy today.
The 10 Golden Verb Laws for SSC Aspirants
Law 1: Do/Does/Did + V1 (Base Form)

Never use V2, V3, or -s/-es after auxiliary do, does, or did. (e.g. did not go, not did not went).

Law 2: Have/Has/Had + V3 (Participle)

Split auxiliaries must supply their own forms: His assistants have done and are doing excellent work.

Law 3: Modal Auxiliary + Bare V1

Active modals take bare V1 (can do, will go). Double modals (will can) are strictly prohibited.

Law 4: Happening Verbs Have NO Passive Voice

Verbs representing natural events without an agent (occur, happen, rain, expire, appear) never take passive forms.

Law 5: Stative Verbs Reject Continuous (-ing)

Verbs of perception/cognition (know, understand, believe, love, own) cannot take continuous aspect in static senses.

Law 6: Need & Dare Dual Nature

As Auxiliaries (negative/question) they take bare V1: She need not go. As Main Verbs they take to + V1: She needs to go.

Law 7: Used to vs Be + Used to

used to + V1 = past discontinued habit. be/get + used to + V4 (-ing) = routine / familiarity.

Law 8: Can/Could vs Able To Ban

Can and could already express capability; combining them with able to creates fatal redundancy.

Law 9: Lest + Should + V1 (No 'Not')

Lest is inherently negative. Never add not. It takes should + V1 or bare subjunctive V1.

Law 10: Transitive Preposition Taboos

Do not insert superfluous prepositions: discuss (no about), comprise (no of), request (no to), approach (no to).

Module 01

Verb Fundamentals & Transitive vs Intransitive Syntax

1. Finite Verb (Tense-Bearing)

A finite verb changes its form based on the Tense, Number (singular/plural), and Person of the subject. It serves as the primary predicate of a complete clause.

• He needs to go there. (Present Finite)
• He needed to go there. (Past Finite)
• They need to go there. (Plural Subject Concord)
2. Non-Finite Verb (Infinite)

A non-finite verb does not change its form with tense or person. It cannot stand alone as the main predicate of a sentence. Three types: Infinitive (to + V1), Gerund (V1+ing as Noun), and Participle (Adjective).

• He wants to go there. (Infinitive)
• Running is a good exercise. (Gerund as Subject)
• I saw a running bus. (Present Participle)
CRITICAL EXAM RULE: Happening Verbs Have NO Passive Voice!

A happening verb describes a spontaneous event or a natural process that happens by itself, without an intentional external doer. Because there is no active agent acting upon an object, they can never be used in the passive voice!

Common Happening Verbs ❌ Incorrect Passive Trap ✔️ Correct Active Form SSC Exam Rationale
Rain, Open, Stop ❌ An accident was occurred. ✔️ An accident occurred. 'Occur' cannot be framed as passive.
Occur, Happen ❌ Such phenomenon is happened. ✔️ Such phenomenon happens. Natural occurrence without human doer.
Expire, Appear ❌ The plan will be expired next week. ✔️ The plan will expire next week. 'Expire' is intransitive happening verb.
Arise, Sleep, Die ❌ A dispute was arisen yesterday. ✔️ A dispute arose yesterday. Use past simple active V2 'arose'.

Transitive ($V_t$) vs Intransitive ($V_i$) Mastery

A Transitive Verb ($V_t$) requires a direct object to complete its meaning. To test for an object, ask the verb two questions: What? (Direct Object) or Whom? (Indirect Object). If the word answers How?, When?, or Where?, it is an Adverb, and the verb is Intransitive ($V_i$).

Transitive Verbs ($V_t$ Takes Object)
  • •
    I taught you. → Answers Whom? (Indirect Object)
  • •
    I taught a lesson. → Answers What? (Direct Object)
  • •
    He broke the window. → Answers What? (Direct Object)
Intransitive Verbs ($V_i$ No Object)
  • •
    I taught nicely. → Answers How? (Adverb of Manner)
  • •
    I taught last month. → Answers When? (Adverb of Time)
  • •
    Sparrows fly high in the sky. → Answers Where? (Adverbial)
Ditransitive Verbs (Two Objects: Indirect + Direct)

Ditransitive verbs take both an Indirect Object (person receiving, answering Whom?) and a Direct Object (thing transferred, answering What?):

• She told me [IO] a nice plan [DO].
• The old woman gave her dog [IO] biscuits [DO].
• Transformed with preposition: The old woman gave biscuits to her dog.
The Superfluous Preposition Trap (Frequent SSC Errors)

Because these verbs are purely transitive, they take direct objects without any intervening prepositions:

❌ approach to minister ✔️ approach minister
❌ demand for help ✔️ demand help
❌ stress on matter ✔️ stress this matter
❌ qualify in exams ✔️ qualify all exams
❌ attack on him ✔️ attack him
❌ afford to that bike ✔️ afford that bike

Module 03

Stative Verbs, Voice & Verb Parallelism

The Stative Law & Conversion Formula

Stative verbs describe conditions, perceptions, mental states, and possessions rather than active physical tasks. They are never used in Continuous or Perfect Continuous tenses in their static sense.

Continuous → Simple Indefinite
❌ I am knowing the answer.
✔️ I know the answer.
Perfect Continuous → Perfect
❌ Had he been knowing about changes...
✔️ Had he known about changes...

5 Core Stative Verb Taxonomies

1. Perception

See, taste, smell, hear, prefer, please

❌ Rose is smelling sweet.
✔️ Rose smells sweet.
2. Thinking & Cognition

Think, know, mean, mind, remember, understand, believe

❌ No one was knowing.
✔️ No one knew.
3. Possession

Own, possess, belong, comprise, contain, have

❌ We are having flats.
✔️ We have flats.
4. Appearance

Look, seem, appear, resemble

❌ Raman is looking smart.
✔️ Raman looks smart.
5. Requirement / State

Cost, require, need, affect, forgive, fit

❌ It is requiring help.
✔️ It requires help.
6. Exception: Gerund & Participle

Stative verbs CAN take -ing as Gerunds or Participles

✔️ Hearing the news, he smiled.
(Participle acting as modifier)
Sense Verbs Require Adjectives, Not Adverbs

Verbs of sensory perception (smell, taste, sound, look, seem, feel) link to a subject complement and must be followed by an Adjective:

❌ tested sweetly
✔️ tested sweet
❌ sounds illogically
✔️ sounds illogical
❌ looks well, works good
✔️ looks good, works well

Verb Parallelism with Coordinating Conjunctions

Clauses coordinated by and, or, or not only... but also must maintain strict parallelism in voice, tense, and form:

Faulty Sentence Parallel Correction Rule Breakdown
❌ She sings beautifully and dance on stage. ✔️ She sings... and dances. Present singular (sings) must balance with present singular (dances).
❌ The committee might have postponed or even cancel the meeting. ✔️ ...postponed or even cancelled. 'Might have' governs both: V3 (postponed) or V3 (cancelled).
❌ He was late for school and punished. ✔️ ...and was punished. Active 'was late' cannot coordinate with bare passive without repeating 'was'.
❌ Not only failed but also faced with criticism. ✔️ ...not only failed but also faced criticism. 'Faced' is transitive taking direct object without 'with'.

Module 04

Modal & Semi-Modal Mastery

Need & Dare: The Semi-Modal Rules

Need and Dare are dual-function verbs. Their syntax depends completely on whether they function as Helping Verbs or Main Verbs:

1. As Auxiliary / Helping Verb

• Used in Negative and Interrogative sentences.

• Followed by bare V1 without 'to'.

• Never takes '-s', '-es', or '-ed'.

✔️ She need not go there.
✔️ Dare he face his father?
❌ She need not to go.
2. As Main Verb

• Used in Affirmative, Negative, or Questions with do/does/did.

• Takes standard verb inflections (needs, dared).

• Followed by to + V1 (Infinitive).

✔️ She needs to help me.
✔️ He dares to face them.
✔️ Does she dare to abuse you?
Used to vs Be + Used to
1. Subject + used to + V1 Expresses discontinued past habit or past state.
He used to play cricket every evening.
2. Subject + be/get + used to + V4 (-ing) Expresses current routine, familiarity, or habituation.
He is used to driving on the left in London.
Can / Could vs Able To Redundancy
The Redundancy Rule Can and Could inherently express ability or capability. Combining them with able to creates a double assertion.
❌ He could not be able to attend.
✔️ He could not attend.
✔️ He was not able to attend.

High-Yield Modal Usages

Would
  • • Polite Offer: Would you like some tea?
  • • Preference: would rather... than + V1
  • • Discontinued Past: He would tell stories.
  • • With like/love/prefer: I would prefer to stay.
May vs Might
  • • May: Strong possibility / formal permission.
  • • Might: Remote / weak possibility.
  • • Purpose (so that / in order that):
    Present → may (works so he may pass)
    Past → might (worked so he might pass)
Lest (Negative Conjunction)
  • • Meaning: "for fear that / in case".
  • • NEVER use 'not' with lest!
  • • Followed by should + V1 (or bare V1):
    Work hard lest you should fail.
    Walk carefully lest you should fall.
The Perfect Modals ($Modal + have + V_3$)
Structure Sense / Meaning Exam Example
Could have + $V_3$ Capability existed in the past, but action was not done. You could have won the first prize, but you didn't try hard enough.
Might have + $V_3$ Past speculation / uncertain guess. He didn't pay the bill; he might have forgotten his wallet.
Must have + $V_3$ Strong past certainty deduced from present evidence. The roads are wet; it must have rained in the night.
Should have + $V_3$ Past obligation that was unfulfilled (criticism / regret). The library is closed now; you should have come earlier.
Needn't have + $V_3$ Action was done in the past, but was completely unnecessary. You needn't have watered the plants; I had already done it.
