Root Etymology: The Latin Verbum ("Word")

In Latin, verbum means "a word". A verb is considered the most essential word in any sentence—without a verb, no complete thought or predicate can exist.

Word Meaning Exam Application / Example
Verb A word showing action, state, or possession He is reading a novel. (Action)
Verbatim Word for word; exactly as spoken or written He reproduced the speech verbatim.
Verbiage Excessive use of unnecessary words The legal draft was packed with pompous verbiage.
Verbose Using more words than necessary; wordy His presentation was too verbose for a quick meeting.
Verbal Expressed in spoken or written words He gave a verbal warning before issuing a memo.
Proverb A short popular saying expressing common truth "A stitch in time saves nine" is a well-known proverb.

The 3 Functional Dimensions of a Verb

A verb does not merely indicate physical action. It defines three distinct semantic relationships:

1. Action (Doing / Happening)

Describes what a subject does, or what naturally takes place.

He is reading a novel.
2. Possession (Ownership)

Shows possession or belonging without performing physical action.

He has a novel.
3. State (Condition / Being)

Links the subject to a complement defining identity or condition.

He is a novelist.

The 10 Golden Verb Laws for SSC

  • Law 1: Do / Does / Did is strictly followed by base verb V1. Never use V2, V3, or -s/-es.
  • Law 2: Have / Has / Had is strictly followed by past participle V3. Never leave split auxiliaries bare.
  • Law 3: Modals (will, shall, can, could, may, might, must, should, would) take bare V1 in active voice and be + V3 in passive voice.
  • Law 4: Happening verbs (occur, happen, rain, expire, appear) describe natural spontaneous events and have NO passive voice.
  • Law 5: Stative verbs (know, believe, understand, resemble, seem, own) represent mental states or perception and cannot be used in Continuous (-ing) tenses.
  • Law 6: Need and Dare are semi-modals: as auxiliaries in negative/interrogative they take bare V1; as main verbs they take to + V1.
  • Law 7: Used to + V1 expresses past habit, whereas be / get + used to + V4 (-ing) expresses current familiarity or routine.
  • Law 8: Can / Could inherently indicates ability; combining it with able to is a fatal redundancy error.
  • Law 9: Lest takes should + V1 (or bare subjunctive V1) and never permits not because it is inherently negative.
  • Law 10: Transitive verbs (approach, demand, stress, attack, afford) take direct objects without intervening prepositions.

Module 1: Verb Fundamentals & Transitive vs Intransitive

Classification, Finite vs Non-Finite, Doing vs Happening, and Preposition Traps

Module 1

Verb Fundamentals & Transitive / Intransitive Classification

Mastering the structural difference between Finite vs Non-Finite, Doing vs Happening, and Transitive vs Intransitive is the bedrock of English syntax.

Finite Verb vs Non-Finite Verb

Finite Verb: Changes its form according to tense, number, and person of the subject. It creates a complete clause.

Non-Finite Verb: Does not change with tense or person (Gerund, Infinitive, Participle). It cannot stand alone as the main predicate.

• He needs (Finite) to go (Non-finite) there.
• He needed (Finite) to go (Non-finite) there.
• Running (Non-finite Gerund) is (Finite) a good exercise.

Action: Doing vs Happening

Doing Verb: Action performed intentionally by an external agent (doer).

Happening Verb: Event or natural process occurring spontaneously by itself without any external agent (e.g. rain, occur, happen, expire, appear, stop).

• The boy stopped the car. (Doing • Transitive)
• The car stopped suddenly. (Happening • Intransitive)
• An accident occurred near the station. (Happening)

🚨 Crucial Rule: Happening Verbs Have NO Passive Voice!

Because a happening verb represents an event with no external doer, it cannot be transformed into the passive voice. Look at these common SSC traps:

Incorrect Sentence Correct Sentence Reason
❌ This plan will be expired next week. ✔️ This plan will expire next week. 'Expire' is a happening verb; no passive.
❌ Such phenomenon is happened once in a century. ✔️ Such phenomenon happens once in a century. 'Happen' is intransitive happening verb.
❌ An accident was occurred near the station. ✔️ An accident occurred near the station. 'Occur' cannot be framed in passive.
❌ A serious problem was arisen in the meeting. ✔️ A serious problem arose in the meeting. 'Arise' is intransitive; use active past V2.

Transitive ($V_t$) vs Intransitive ($V_i$) Verbs

A Transitive Verb ($V_t$) requires an object to complete its meaning. Ask the verb What? (Direct Object) or Whom? (Indirect Object).

An Intransitive Verb ($V_i$) does NOT take an object. If a word follows an intransitive verb answering How?, When?, or Where?, it is an Adverb or a Prepositional Phrase.

Sentence Verb Type Function of Following Word
I taught you. Transitive ($V_t$) 'You' answers Whom? → Indirect Object
I taught a lesson. Transitive ($V_t$) 'A lesson' answers What? → Direct Object
I taught last month. Intransitive ($V_i$) 'Last month' answers When? → Adverb of Time
I taught there. Intransitive ($V_i$) 'There' answers Where? → Adverb of Place
I taught nicely. Intransitive ($V_i$) 'Nicely' answers How? → Adverb of Manner

Ditransitive Verbs (Two Objects)

A ditransitive verb takes both an Indirect Object (person receiving, answering Whom?) and a Direct Object (thing given, answering What?):

She told me (Indirect Object) a nice plan (Direct Object).

The old woman gave her dog (Indirect Object) biscuits (Direct Object).

Alternative order with preposition: The old woman gave biscuits (Direct Object) to her dog (Prepositional Object).

Verbs That Take Direct Objects Without Superfluous Prepositions

SSC frequently tests errors where superfluous prepositions are inserted between a transitive verb and its direct object:

  • ❌ The man approached to the minister. → ✔️ approached the minister.
  • ❌ He will demand for more help. → ✔️ demand more help.
  • ❌ He should stress on this matter. → ✔️ stress this matter.
  • ❌ She has qualified in all the exams. → ✔️ qualified all the exams.
  • ❌ He should not attack on him. → ✔️ attack him.
  • ❌ She cannot afford to that bike. → ✔️ afford that bike.
  • ❌ She didn't like in the new project. → ✔️ like the new project.
  • ❌ The cyclone caused the broken window. → ✔️ The cyclone broke the window.

Module 2: High-Yield Confusing Verb Pairs & Triads

Rise vs Raise, Lie vs Lay, Fall vs Fell, Invariant Verbs & Form Irregularities

Module 2

High-Yield Confusing Verb Pairs & Triads

This section contains the single highest-tested category in SSC English exams: phonetically similar or semantically related verbs with vastly different transitivity and forms.

1. The Quad: Rise • Raise • Arise • Arouse

Verb Type Forms ($V_1 - V_2 - V_3$) Meaning Usage Example
Rise Intransitive ($V_i$) Rise → Rose → Risen To get up, ascend, grow, increase The sun rises in the east. Prices have risen sharply.
Raise Transitive ($V_t$) Raise → Raised → Raised To lift up, elevate something (requires object) He raised his hand. The discovery raised hopes.
Arise Intransitive ($V_i$) Arise → Arose → Arisen To emerge, come into notice, occur A serious conflict arose during negotiations.
Arouse Transitive ($V_t$) Arouse → Aroused → Aroused To evoke feelings, excite curiosity/anger His suspicious behavior aroused doubts.

2. The Ultimate Confusing Triad: Lie vs Lay vs Lie (Untruth)

Verb Type $V_1$ (Base) $V_2$ (Past) $V_3$ (Participle) $V_4$ (-ing) Meaning
Lie Intransitive ($V_i$) Lie Lay Lain Lying To recline, rest horizontally on a surface
Lay Transitive ($V_t$) Lay Laid Laid Laying To place/put an object down; establish; sacrifice
Lie Intransitive ($V_i$) Lie Lied Lied Lying To tell a falsehood / speak untruth
• He lay on the ground because he was tired. (Past of recline → Lay)
• He laid some books on the table. (Past of place something → Laid + Object)
• Lay the guns down and lie down on the floor. (Lay = place object; Lie = recline)
• He lied to his father many times. (Untruth → Lied)

3. Fall vs Fell vs Befall

Verb Type Forms ($V_1 - V_2 - V_3$) Meaning Example
Fall Intransitive ($V_i$) Fall → Fell → Fallen To drop down by gravity / tumble He fell off the ladder. Leaves fall in autumn.
Fell Transitive ($V_t$) Fell → Felled → Felled To cut down (trees); strike down The woodcutter felled many ancient trees.
Befall Transitive ($V_t$) Befall → Befell → Befallen (Of tragedy/misfortune) to happen to A terrible tragedy befell the entire village.
Find vs Found

Find: Find → Found → Found (to discover/obtain).

Found: Found → Founded → Founded (to establish/set up).

The university was founded in 1950.
Sink vs Drown

Sink: Sink → Sank → Sunk (for non-living objects / ships).

Drown: Drown → Drowned → Drowned (for living beings dying in water).

The ship sank; the sailors drowned.
Hung vs Hanged

Hung: Hang → Hung → Hung (suspending clothes, paintings, pictures).

Hanged: Hang → Hanged → Hanged (execution / capital punishment).

He hung his coat; the convict was hanged.

Module 3: Stative Verbs, Voice & Parallelism

Cognition/Perception Categories, Sensory Shifts, Correlative Parallelism, and Adjective Participles

Module 3

Stative Verbs, Voice & Verb Parallelism

Stative verbs describe conditions, perceptions, mental states, and possessions rather than active physical tasks. Understanding when and why they reject continuous aspect is vital for SSC scoring.

The Fundamental Rule of Stative Verbs

Golden Conversion Rule:
• If a stative verb is used in the Continuous Tense, change it to the Simple Indefinite Tense.
• If a stative verb is used in the Perfect Continuous Tense, change it to the Perfect Tense.
Category Stative Verbs Error Example & Correction
Perception / Sensation See, taste, smell, hear, prefer, please ❌ The rose is smelling sweet.
✔️ The rose smells sweet.
Thinking / Cognition Think, know, mean, mind, remember, understand, believe ❌ No one was knowing the answer.
✔️ No one knew the answer.
Possession Own, possess, belong, comprise, contain, have ❌ We are having three flats.
✔️ We have three flats.
Appearance Look, seem, appear, resemble ❌ Raman is looking smart.
✔️ Raman looks smart.
State / Requirement Cost, require, need, affect, forgive, fit ❌ This book is costing Rs 500.
✔️ This book costs Rs 500.

Stative vs Dynamic Dual Nature

Certain verbs function as stative in one sense, but dynamic (action) in another. When describing an active process, they can take -ing:

Smell

• State (emits odor): The flower smells sweet.

• Action (active sniffing): He is smelling the flower.

Have

• State (possession): I have a car.

• Action (eating/discussing): We are having lunch.

Think

• State (opinion/belief): I think he is honest.

• Action (mental process): I am thinking about my future.

Look

• State (appearance): She looks gorgeous.

• Action (gazing at): She is looking at the painting.

Sense Verbs Take Adjectives, Action Verbs Take Adverbs

Verbs of sensation (smell, taste, sound, look, seem, feel, appear) describe the state of the subject and must be modified by an Adjective, not an Adverb:

Incorrect Sentence Correct Sentence Explanation
❌ This fruit tested sweetly. ✔️ This fruit tested sweet. Sense verb 'taste' takes adjective complement 'sweet'.
❌ His argument sounds illogically. ✔️ His argument sounds illogical. Sense verb 'sound' takes adjective 'illogical'.
❌ The machine looks well but does not work good. ✔️ The machine looks good but does not work well. 'Look' takes adjective 'good'; action verb 'work' takes adverb 'well'.

Verb Parallelism with Conjunctions (And, Or, Not only... but also)

Both sides of coordinating conjunctions must maintain syntactic balance in verb tense, voice (active/passive), and form:

Sentence & Error Correction Grammatical Parallelism Rule
❌ She sings beautifully and dance on stage. ✔️ She sings... and dances Singular present 'sings' must match singular present 'dances'.
❌ The committee might have postponed or even cancel the meeting. ✔️ ...might have postponed or even cancelled 'Might have' governs both verbs: V3 (postponed) or V3 (cancelled).
❌ He was late for school and punished. ✔️ He was late for school and was punished. Active 'was late' cannot seamlessly coordinate with passive 'punished' without repeating the passive auxiliary.
❌ The proposal not only failed but also faced with criticism. ✔️ ...not only failed but also faced widespread criticism. 'Faced' is transitive taking direct object without 'with'.

Past Participle Adjectives vs $V_3$ Verbs

In English, several irregular verbs have distinct forms for their verbal past participle ($V_3$) and their descriptive adjective:

Base Verb ($V_1$) Past Participle ($V_3$) Participial Adjective Correct Usage Comparison
Shear Sheared Shorn He sheared the sheep. vs These are shorn sheep.
Prove Proved Proven Newton has proved it. vs It is a proven fact.
Sink Sunk Sunken The ship had sunk. vs They found a sunken ship.
Drink Drunk Drunken He had drunk water. vs He was in a drunken state.

Module 4: Modals & Semi-Modals

Need & Dare, Used to, Would/May/Might, Lest Conjunction, and Perfect Modals

Module 4

Modal & Semi-Modal Mastery

Modals express mood, possibility, obligation, permission, and necessity. They follow rigorous syntactic rules that are tested in every SSC examination.

Fundamental Modal Properties

  • Modals are helping verbs that express modality (ability, obligation, possibility).
  • Modals are strictly followed by bare $V_1$ in active voice.
  • Modals never take inflections: no -s, -es, -ed, or -ing.
  • Double modals cannot be used together (e.g. ❌ He will can do it).
  • In passive: Modal + be + V_3.
  • In past perfect: Modal + have + V_3.

Need & Dare: The Semi-Modals

As Auxiliary (Helping Verb):
Used in negative and interrogative sentences. Takes bare $V_1$ without 'to'. No '-s/-es'.

She need not go there. / Dare he face his father?

As Main Verb:
Takes ordinary verb forms (needs, dared) and takes to + V_1 infinitive.

She needs to go there. / He dares to face his father.

🚨 Need & Dare Exam Drill Breakdown

Sentence Correct? Explanation
1. She needs help me. ❌ Incorrect 'Needs' is main verb; requires infinitive → She needs to help me.
2. She need not to help me. ❌ Incorrect 'Need' is auxiliary; requires bare $V_1$ without 'to' → She need not help me.
3. She dare not to face her father. ❌ Incorrect 'Dare' is auxiliary; takes bare $V_1$ → She dare not face her father.
4. She dares to face her father. ✔️ Correct 'Dares' is affirmative main verb taking to + V_1.
5. Does she dares to abuse you? ❌ Incorrect Auxiliary 'Does' requires base form → Does she dare to abuse you?

Used to vs Be + Used to

1. Subject + used to + $V_1$:
Expresses a discontinued past habit or past state.

He used to smoke when he was young.

2. Subject + be/get + used to + $V_4 (-ing)$:
Expresses routine, familiarity, or habituation in present or past.

He is used to driving on the left in the UK.

Can / Could vs Able To Redundancy

'Can' and 'Could' already denote capability or ability. Using them together with 'able to' creates an ungrammatical redundancy:

❌ He could not be able to attend.
✔️ He could not attend. (or: was not able to attend)
❌ I will not able to come.
✔️ I will not be able to come.

High-Yield Modal Rules: Would • May / Might • Lest

Modal Primary Functions & Structures Examples
Would • Polite offer: Would you like...?
• Preference: would rather + V_1 ... than + V_1
• Past discontinued habit: My grandfather would tell us stories.
• Conditional wish: like / love / prefer 		Would you like a cup of tea?
I would rather die than surrender.
I would prefer to stay at home tonight.
May / Might • Possibility: May (strong) vs Might (remote)
• Optative sentence: May God bless you!
• Purpose with so that / in order that:
Present main clause → may
Past main clause → might 		He works hard so that he may succeed.
He worked hard so that he might succeed.
Lest • Meaning: "for fear that" / "in case"
• Inherently negative (NEVER use 'not')
• Takes should + V_1 (or bare subjunctive V_1) 		Work hard lest you should fail.
Walk carefully lest you should fall.

The Perfect Modals ($Modal + have + V_3$)

Used for evaluating past actions with counterfactual, speculative, or obligatory perspectives:

Structure Sense / Meaning Exam Example
Could have + $V_3$ Capability existed in the past, but the action was not done. You could have won the first prize, but you didn't try hard enough.
Might have + $V_3$ Past possibility or speculation based on incomplete facts. He didn't pay the bill; he might have forgotten his wallet.
Must have + $V_3$ Strong certainty about past events based on clear present evidence. The streets are wet; it must have rained last night.
Should have + $V_3$ Past obligation or duty that was NOT fulfilled (regret/criticism). The library is closed now; you should have come earlier.
Needn't have + $V_3$ An action was performed in the past, but it was completely unnecessary. You needn't have watered the plants; I had already done it.

Practice Arena — Set 1 (Questions 1 to 33)

Error Spotting Drills with Active Recall & Complete Explanations

Exam PYQ • Confusing Verb Q1
It was the misalignment between projected and actual data (1)/ that cast doubt not only on the calculations (2)/ but also on the assumptions that (3)/ underlied the funding strategy. (4)
Error Location: Part (4)
Correction: Replace 'underlied' with 'underlay'.
Grammar Rule: The base verb is 'underlie' (V1: underlie, V2: underlay, V3: underlain). In simple past tense, the correct form is 'underlay'. 'Underlied' is an incorrect regularized form.
Key Formula: Underlie (V1) → Underlay (V2) → Underlain (V3)
Irregular Verb Forms Q2
The farmer cut his (a)/ leg with a sickle (b)/ and bleed profusely. (c)/ No error (d)
Error Location: Part (c)
Correction: Replace 'bleed' with 'bled'.
Grammar Rule: The sentence narrating a past incident uses past tense 'cut' (V2). Hence the coordinate verb joined by 'and' must also be in past tense (V2): 'bled'. (V1: bleed, V2: bled, V3: bled).
Key Formula: Bleed (V1) → Bled (V2) → Bled (V3)
Sentence Selection Q3
Choose the grammatically correct sentence:
(a) When the gardener went to water the plants, he digged a small pit near the gate.
(b) When the gardener went to water the plants, he dug a small pit near the gate.
(c) When the gardener gone to water the plants, he dug a small pit near the gate.
(d) When the gardener was went to water the plants, he digged a small pit near the gate.
Error Location: Option (b)
Correction: Option (b) is grammatically correct.
Grammar Rule: The verb 'dig' has irregular past forms: V1: dig, V2: dug, V3: dug. 'Digged' does not exist in English. In (c), 'gone' is V3 and cannot stand without an auxiliary. In (d), 'was went' is invalid.
Key Formula: Dig (V1) → Dug (V2) → Dug (V3)
Irregular Verb Forms Q4
When the meeting (a)/ was over, the participants torn up (b)/ the documents they had used during the discussion. (c)/ No error (d)
Error Location: Part (b)
Correction: Replace 'torn up' with 'tore up'.
Grammar Rule: 'Torn' is the past participle (V3) and requires an auxiliary verb (has/have/had). For past simple, use the V2 form 'tore'.
Key Formula: Tear (V1) → Tore (V2) → Torn (V3)
Happening Verb • Passive Error Q5
All these documents (1)/ have been destroyed (2)/ in the fire (3)/ which were occurred last month. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (4)
Correction: Replace 'which were occurred' with 'which occurred'.
Grammar Rule: 'Occur' is an intransitive happening verb (a spontaneous natural event without an external human agent). Happening verbs have no passive voice.
Key Formula: Happening Verbs (occur, happen, rain, arise) → NEVER used in Passive Voice!
Auxiliary + Verb Form Q6
Haven't you notice (1)/ that for the past one month (2)/ he hasn't been here (3)/ at his regular place? (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (1)
Correction: Replace 'notice' with 'noticed'.
Grammar Rule: The helping verb 'have/has/had' always requires the past participle form (V3). Hence, 'Haven't you noticed'.
Key Formula: Have / Has / Had + V3
Finite Verb Requirement Q7
It seemed to him (1)/ that the manager of the (2)/ bank looking at him (3)/ in a rather suspicious manner. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (3)
Correction: Replace 'looking at him' with 'was looking at him' or 'looked at him'.
Grammar Rule: A subordinate clause introduced by 'that' must have a complete finite verb predicate. The bare participle 'looking' lacks an auxiliary.
Key Formula: Clause Predicate = Subject + Auxiliary + V4 (or simple V2)
Irregular Verb Forms Q8
A group of youngsters (1)/ has lend financial assistance (2)/ to the flood affected (3)/ people of the villages. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (2)
Correction: Replace 'has lend' with 'has lent'.
Grammar Rule: The auxiliary 'has' requires the past participle (V3) 'lent'. (V1: lend, V2: lent, V3: lent).
Key Formula: Lend (V1) → Lent (V2) → Lent (V3)
Do/Does/Did + V1 Rule Q9
Having failed to get (1)/ good marks in (2)/ her first attempt (3)/ she did never tried again. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (4)
Correction: Replace 'did never tried' with 'never tried' (or 'did never try').
Grammar Rule: The auxiliary 'did' takes base form V1 ('did never try'). But idiomatically, 'never' alone suffices with simple past: 'she never tried again'.
Key Formula: Did + V1 (Base Form)
Modal Passive Q10
This book may not be reproduce (1)/ in whole or in part in any form (2)/ without the written permission (3)/ of the publishers. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (1)
Correction: Replace 'reproduce' with 'reproduced'.
Grammar Rule: In passive modal structures, 'Modal + be' must be followed by the past participle (V3): 'may not be reproduced'.
Key Formula: Modal + be + V3 (Passive Voice)
Modal Parallelism Q11
Such programmes will lead to (1)/ an exchange of ideas and (2)/ will create a better understanding (3)/ between the two countries. (4)/ No Error (5)
Error Location: Part (5)
Correction: No error.
Grammar Rule: Both coordinate clauses use modal + V1 correctly: 'will lead' and 'will create'. The sentence is completely grammatical.
Key Formula: Modal + V1 in active voice
Modal + V1 Rule Q12
The committee has made (1)/ significant changes in (2)/ the rules which (3)/ will helped the banks. (4)/ No Error (5)
Error Location: Part (4)
Correction: Replace 'will helped' with 'will help'.
Grammar Rule: Modals (will, shall, can, could, may, might, must, should) are always followed by the base form (V1), never by V2 or V3.
Key Formula: Modal + V1 (help, not helped)
Lie vs Lay Confusing Pair Q13
The doctor advised Mr. Murugan that, (1)/ because of his severe cramps (2)/ he should lay in the bed for a few days. (3)/ No Error (4)
Error Location: Part (3)
Correction: Replace 'lay' with 'lie'.
Grammar Rule: 'Lie' is intransitive meaning to recline/rest (V1: lie, V2: lay, V3: lain). 'Lay' is transitive meaning to put/place something (takes object). After modal 'should', we need the base form of recline: 'lie'.
Key Formula: Lie (Vi, recline) = Lie, Lay, Lain | Lay (Vt, place) = Lay, Laid, Laid
Did + V1 Rule Q14
Since the trip home (1)/ was expensive I (2)/ did not went home (3)/ during the holidays. (4)/ No Error (5)
Error Location: Part (3)
Correction: Replace 'did not went' with 'did not go'.
Grammar Rule: After 'did not', the main verb must always remain in base form (V1): 'did not go'.
Key Formula: Did + not + V1 (go, not went)
Passive Voice Form Q15
Palm oil is very beneficial (1)/ and is use to (2)/ make products ranging (3)/ from soap to biodiesel. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (2)
Correction: Replace 'is use to' with 'is used to'.
Grammar Rule: Passive construction requires 'be (is) + V3': 'is used to make'.
Key Formula: Be + V3 (used)
Rise vs Raise Confusing Pair Q16
The data shows that (1)/ the unemployment rate has (2)/ raised to 6.1% (3)/ the highest in five years. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (3)
Correction: Replace 'raised' with 'risen'.
Grammar Rule: 'Rise' is an intransitive verb meaning to go up / increase on its own without an external agent (V1: rise, V2: rose, V3: risen). 'Raise' is transitive and requires a direct object. Since no object follows, use 'risen'.
Key Formula: Rise (Vi) → Rose → Risen (No object) | Raise (Vt) → Raised → Raised (Takes object)
Infinitive to + V1 Q17
He has taken care to (1)/ compliance with the norms (2)/ so he expects (3)/ the proposal to be approved without delay (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (2)
Correction: Replace 'compliance with' with 'comply with'.
Grammar Rule: 'To' in an infinitive must be followed by the base verb (V1). 'Compliance' is an abstract noun; its verb form is 'comply'.
Key Formula: To + V1 (comply with = obey)
Did + V1 Rule Q18
As the salary (1)/ did not match (2)/ his expectations, he did not (3)/ accepted the job. (4)/ No error (5)
Error Location: Part (4)
Correction: Replace 'accepted' with 'accept'.
Grammar Rule: Auxiliary 'did not' takes the bare base form (V1): 'did not accept'.
Key Formula: Did not + V1
