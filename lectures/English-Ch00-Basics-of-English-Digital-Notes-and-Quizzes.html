0. Letters, Sounds & Hierarchy of English Units

Page 2 • The Smallest Unit of Language, Phonetics, Vowel Sounds vs Consonant Sounds, Alphabet vs Alphabets

🔤 1. The Hierarchy of Language Units

Every language starts from the smallest unit of sound and builds up to complex thought expression:

LETTER < WORD < PHRASE < CLAUSE < SENTENCE

Smallest Unit = Letter (26 Letters)

The English alphabet contains 26 written letters divided into:

  • Vowels (5): a, e, i, o, u
  • Consonants (21): b, c, d, f, g, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, q, r, s, t, v, w, x, y, z

Meaningful Group = Word

A word is a meaningful combination of letters. Random combinations without meaning do not form words!

  • Go ✅ | Og ❌ (Meaningless)
  • Come ✅ | Omec ❌ (Meaningless)
  • Hindi analogy: क + म + ल = कमल ✅ | म + क + ल = मकल ❌

🗣️ 2. Phonetics & Sounds (44 Sounds in English)

While English has 26 written letters, it produces 44 phonetic sounds:

20 Vowel Sounds

Divided into pure sounds and gliding sounds:

  • Monophthongs (12): Pure Vowels / Single Vowel sounds (एकल स्वर ध्वनि).
  • Diphthongs (8): Double Vowel sounds / Glides (द्विस्वर ध्वनि).

24 Consonant Sounds

Produced with partial or complete closure of the vocal tract (व्यंजन ध्वनियाँ).

📢 The Article Sound Rule (A vs An):

Articles A / An depend strictly on the INITIAL PHONETIC SOUND, not the written letter:

  • An Hour: Begins with vowel sound 'आ' ➔ takes An.
  • A Horse: Begins with consonant sound 'ह' ➔ takes A.
  • An Eagle: Begins with vowel sound 'इ' ➔ takes An.
  • A European: Begins with consonant sound 'य' (yu-) ➔ takes A.

⚠️ 3. Alphabet vs Alphabets (High-Frequency Exam Trap - Page 2)

Alphabet (वर्णमाला)

The entire set of letters (A to Z) arranged in a fixed order. It is an uncountable singular noun for a single language.

• "Children learn the alphabet before they start reading words." ✅

• "The teacher wrote the alphabet on the board." ✅

Alphabets = Languages (भाषाएँ)

When 's' is added to alphabet, its meaning changes completely to LANGUAGES!

• "He knows many alphabets." (Means he knows many languages).

• "He learned 26 alphabets" ❌ ➔ "26 letters of the alphabet" ✅.

1. The 8 Parts of Speech & Form-Function Attributes

Page 2 • Identification of Words in Context, The 8 Functional Categories, Grammatical Attributes

🏷️ 1. The 8 Parts of Speech

Every single word in an English sentence performs a specific syntactic role known as its Part of Speech:

1. NOUN: Naming word (Rohan, honesty, team) 2. PRONOUN: Replaces noun (they, he, which) 3. VERB: Action/State/Possession (go, come, is, have) 4. ADJECTIVE: Qualifies noun/pronoun (intelligent, brave) 5. ADVERB: Modifies verb/adj/adverb (very, wisely, fast) 6. PREPOSITION: Relational word (on, in, between, for) 7. CONJUNCTION: Connects words/clauses (and, but, because) 8. INTERJECTION: Sudden emotion (Alas!, Hurrah!, Wow!)

Note: Modern functional grammar also recognizes Articles (a, an, the) and Determiners (this, that, some, few) as foundational word classes.

⚙️ 2. The 6 Attributes of Words (Form & Function)

AttributeCategories / ValuesApplicable Parts of SpeechExample
1. Number Singular / Plural Noun, Pronoun, Verb Boy → Boys | He → They | Plays → Play
2. Gender Masculine, Feminine, Common, Neuter Noun, Pronoun Actor / Actress | He / She / It
3. Cases Subjective (Nominative), Objective (Accusative), Possessive (Genitive) Noun, Pronoun He (Subject) → Him (Object) → His (Possessive)
4. Person 1st Person (Speaker), 2nd Person (Listener), 3rd Person (Spoken about) Pronouns & Verbs I / We (1st) | You (2nd) | He / She / They (3rd)

2. Inflection vs Non-Inflection in English

Pages 2 & 3 • Which Words Change Form and Which Remain Rigid; The 5 Inflected Classes

🔄 1. What is Inflection (रूप-परिवर्तन)?

Inflection is the change in the form of a word to express a grammatical function such as tense, number, case, gender, or degree of comparison.

5 Inflected Parts of Speech (Change Form)

  • NOUN: Changes for number & possession.
  • PRONOUN: Changes for case, person, and number.
  • VERB: Changes for tense, voice, aspect, participle.
  • ADJECTIVE: Changes for degrees of comparison.
  • ADVERB: Changes for degrees of comparison.

Non-Inflected Parts of Speech (Rigid Form)

These classes NEVER change their spelling or form regardless of sentence context:

  • Preposition: on, in, at, between (Never "ons" or "ined" ❌)
  • Conjunction: and, but, because, although
  • Interjection: alas, hurrah, wow, ouch
  • Article & Determiner: the, every, each

3. Noun & Pronoun — Definitions, Attributes & Syntactic Positions

Page 3 • The 5 Positions of a Noun; The 3 Positions of a Pronoun; Case Harmony

👥 1. What is a Noun? ("Name is Noun")

"Everything is a noun, or Name is a noun." A noun is the naming word for any person, place, thing, concept, emotion, or collection.

Honesty (Abstract) Jury (Collective) Gold (Material) Police (Collective) Childhood (State) Beauty (Quality) Committee (Collective)

📍 The 5 Syntactic Positions of a Noun (Page 3)

Position / Syntactic RoleRule DescriptionMaster Example
1. Noun as Subject Performs the action or is the topic of the sentence. The students are preparing for competitive examinations.
2. Noun as Object of a Verb Directly receives the action of a transitive verb. The teacher explained the lesson.
3. Noun as Object of a Preposition Follows a preposition to complete prepositional phrase. He is interested in music. ('in' = Prep, 'music' = Obj of Prep)
4. Noun as Subject Complement Follows a linking verb to identify/rename the subject. Ram is a teacher. ('is' = Linking verb, 'teacher' = Complement)
5. Noun in Apposition Placed next to another noun to explain or describe it. Kalidas, a great Sanskrit poet, wrote Abhijnanashakuntalam.

🎭 2. What is a Pronoun? ("Represents a Noun")

A pronoun replaces or represents a noun to avoid monotonous repetition.

Classification of Pronouns (Page 3)

  • Personal: I, we, you, he, she, it, they
  • Possessive: mine, ours, yours, his, hers, theirs
  • Self (Reflexive/Emphatic): myself, yourself, himself
  • Demonstrative: this, that, these, those
  • Relative: who, whom, whose, which, that
  • Indefinite: someone, anyone, everybody, none
  • Distributive: each, either, neither

The 3 Syntactic Positions of a Pronoun

  1. Pronoun as Subject:
    She teaches English grammar.
  2. Pronoun as Object of a Verb:
    The principal rewarded them.
  3. Pronoun as Object of a Preposition:
    Between you and me, this decision is incorrect.
⚠️ Golden Exam Trap: Preposition Takes OBJECTIVE Case!
Prepositions (between, for, with, to) are always followed by the Objective Case of a pronoun:
• Between you and I ❌ ➔ Between you and me ✅.
• For he and I ❌ ➔ For him and me ✅.

4. Verb Anatomy — Action, State, Possession & Helping Verbs

Pages 3 & 4 • The 3 Core Functions of Verbs; Main Verbs vs Helping Verbs; Primary vs Modal Auxiliaries

⚡ 1. The 3 Core Functions of a Verb

Traditional definitions say "verb is an action word". But PSP Sir emphasizes: A verb expresses Action, State, OR Possession!

1. Action (कार्य)

Physical or mental dynamic activity.

• "You are playing well."

• "He is driving his car."

2. State (अवस्था / स्थिति)

State of being, identity, condition.

• "You are a good player."

• "He is a driver."

3. Possession (अधिकार)

Ownership or belonging.

• "You have a bat."

• "He has a car."

🔄 Action Verbs: Doing vs Happening (Page 3)

• Doing (Intentionally done by agent): "I am reading a lesson."

• Happening (Occurs naturally without deliberate agent): "It rained last night."

🛠️ 2. Main Verb vs Auxiliary (Helping) Verb

Main Verb (Principal Verb)

  • Possesses forms (V1, V2, V3, V4, V5).
  • Can stand alone as the sole verb in a sentence.
  • Expresses the main action or state.
V1: Base / Present (go, play) V2: Past Simple (went, played) V3: Past Participle (gone, played) V4: Present Participle / -ing (going)

Auxiliary Verb (Helping Verb)

  • Does not have full independent lexical meaning alone.
  • Helps the main verb express tense, mood, passive voice, questions, and negatives.
  • "You are playing." (are = HV, playing = MV)
  • "He has finished his work." (has = HV, finished = MV)

🧭 3. Primary Auxiliaries vs Modal Auxiliaries (Page 4)

Auxiliary CategoryVerbs IncludedSpecial Behavioral Rule
Primary Auxiliaries • Be: is, am, are, was, were
• Do: do, does, did
• Have: has, have, had		 Can act as BOTH Helping Verb AND Main Verb! (e.g. "He has finished" [HV] vs "He has a car" [MV]).
Modal Auxiliaries can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to NEVER act as main verbs alone; always require a base verb (V1).
Semi-Modals (Marginal) Need, Dare Can act as Helping Verbs (in negatives/questions) and also as Main Verbs!

5. Linking Verbs & The "Be" Substitution Trick

Page 4 • Helping Verb vs Linking Verb; Verbs of Sense; The Master Substitution Rule

🔗 1. What is a Linking Verb?

A Linking Verb does not express action. Instead, it connects the subject to a Subject Complement (noun or adjective) that describes or identifies the subject.

The Be-Form Rule (Page 4):
  • If a separate verb follows a "be" form ➔ The "be" form acts as a Helping Verb.
    "You are learning a lesson." ('are' = HV, 'learning' = MV).
  • If NO separate verb follows the "be" form ➔ The "be" form acts as the Main Verb (Linking Verb).
    "You are a learner." ('are' = Linking Verb, 'learner' = Complement).
    "You were there." ('were' = Linking Verb).

👃 2. Verbs of Sensation (Sensory Linking Verbs)

Common linking verbs other than "be": be, seem, become, appear, look, feel, taste, smell, sound, grow, turn, keep, prove.

  • "She is intelligent." (is = LV, intelligent = Adj complement)
  • "He became a doctor." (became = LV, doctor = Noun complement)
  • "The food tastes delicious." (tastes = LV, delicious = Adj complement)

💡 3. The Master Identification Test (The "Be" Substitution Trick - Page 4)

How do you confirm whether a sensory verb is functioning as a Linking Verb?

Replace the verb with a "BE" form (is / was / are). If the sentence remains meaningful and keeps its core sense, the verb is a LINKING VERB!
Original SentenceSubstitute with "Be" (is / was)Result & Conclusion
"It tasted better." ➔ "It was better." Meaningful ➔ 'Tasted' is a Linking Verb!
"He looks happy." ➔ "He is happy." Meaningful ➔ 'Looks' is a Linking Verb!
"He feels sad." ➔ "He is sad." Meaningful ➔ 'Feels' is a Linking Verb!
⚠️ Fundamental SSC Exam Rule:
Linking verbs connect to a Subject Complement (ADJECTIVE), NEVER an adverb!
• "He feels badly" ❌ ➔ "He feels bad" ✅.
• "The rose smells sweetly" ❌ ➔ "The rose smells sweet" ✅.
• "She looked beautifully" ❌ ➔ "She looked beautiful" ✅.
